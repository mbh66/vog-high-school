Ryan Orbuch's essay project (Lowercarbon Capital, published September 2026, at naturalgeneralintelligence.ai), subtitled *The case for a nature model that learns the state and dynamics of the planet itself*. NGI's core scientific claim is that the Earth is a single, tightly coupled system in which the biosphere, atmosphere, oceans, land, ice, and subsurface interact, and life actively maintains the conditions that make life possible. Orbuch's phrasing: natural resources "are produced only by the Earth system itself." The essay draws on James Lovelock's insight that habitability is established through feedback.

NGI is used in the proposed [[silverwood-school|Silverwood]] and [[valley-of-grace-learning-campus|Learning Campus]] curricula as a reference frame for [[phenomenology|phenomenological]] science teaching: it supplies a contemporary, rigorous framing of the whole living Earth as a system, which Waldorf science already begins from. Applied to [[caps|CAPS]] Life Sciences and Geography it reframes without displacing content, since CAPS already requires the study of ecosystems, biodiversity, nutrient cycles, atmosphere, water systems, and human impact.

The caution named in the [[waldorf-caps|Waldorf and CAPS position paper]]: NGI is an argumentative essay, and parts of it are explicitly forward-looking and speculative. It should be taught as a strong argument to be examined, which is exactly the critical posture the upper school wants to build. It cannot substitute for CAPS content in an examined subject.
