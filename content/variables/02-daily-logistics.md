The catchment constraint is the defining feature of the valley's high school problem. There are too few high school pupils in the Greyton/Genadendal corridor to sustain a conventional new English medium school, and every option outside the immediate valley introduces a transport burden.

This variable asks: how does your child physically get to school each day, and what does that cost in money, time, and family capacity?

## The options

### Walking distance (in-valley schools)

**Emil Weder Secondary, Genadendal.** Reachable on foot or by short drive from Greyton (~7 km) and walking distance from Genadendal itself. The only operational high school in the valley. Afrikaans-medium (see [[Variable 1: Language of Instruction]]).

**Silverwood School (if constituted).** The BioHub Founding Brief places Silverwood at Genadendal as part of the Learning Hub. If it opens there, the logistics would be similar to Emil Weder. If it were located on the contested Erf 595 land in Greyton, it would be walking distance for Greyton children. The location question is unresolved and politically sensitive (the R100 land sale controversy documented in the Greyton Commonage Research Report). Parents should treat Silverwood's location as uncertain until a site is confirmed.

**What this costs:** Minimal. A short drive or bicycle ride. No boarding fees. The child sleeps at home.

**What this requires:** That the parent accepts the school on offer in the valley, with its language, curriculum, and pedagogical character.

### Daily transport to Caledon (~35 km each way)

Two public high schools operate in Caledon: Hoërskool Overberg (Afrikaans-medium) and Overberg High School (English-medium, ~492 learners). The drive from Greyton is roughly 35 to 40 minutes via the R406.

**What this costs:**

- Fuel: approximately 70 km round trip per day. At current fuel prices (roughly R25/litre, ~8 km/litre for a standard car), that is roughly R220 per day, or R4,400 per month over 20 school days. Over a 200-day school year, roughly R44,000 in fuel alone. A more fuel-efficient vehicle or a diesel lowers this, but the order of magnitude holds.
- Time: the driver loses roughly 80 to 90 minutes per day. Over a school year, that is approximately 300 hours. If a parent drives, this is time unavailable for work.
- Vehicle wear: tyres, services, and depreciation add to the real cost but are harder to quantify per trip.

**Lift clubs.** If three or four families share the driving, the fuel cost per family drops to a third or a quarter, and each family drives one or two days per week. This is the most common arrangement in rural areas. It requires coordination, reliability, and fallback plans for days when the assigned driver is unavailable.

**Dedicated transport.** A private minibus or scholar transport service running a daily Greyton-to-Caledon route would spread the cost across all users and remove the driving burden from individual parents. Whether such a service exists or could be viable depends on the number of families prepared to commit. If 15 to 20 children were using the route, the per-child monthly cost would likely sit between R1,500 and R3,000 depending on the operator. **TBV**: no current operator has been identified for this specific route.

**What this requires:** Reliable transport every school day for five years. A plan for mornings, afternoons, sports practices, and after-school activities. A plan for when the car breaks down or the lift club fails. Willingness to accept the road risk of a mountain pass commute in winter weather.

### Weekly boarding

Some schools in the broader region offer weekly boarding: the child stays at the school hostel from Monday to Friday and comes home on weekends. Hoërskool Overberg has hostels (referenced on the school's website). Whether Overberg High School offers boarding is **TBV**.

**What this costs:** Boarding fees vary widely. A government school hostel in a rural town typically charges between R20,000 and R40,000 per year. This is a rough range; the actual figure for Hoërskool Overberg or Overberg High School needs to be confirmed directly with the schools.

**What this requires:** The child lives away from home during the week. For a 13-year-old entering Grade 8, this is a significant transition. Some children thrive in a boarding environment. Others do not. The parent's assessment of their own child's readiness matters more than any general rule.

### Full boarding (further afield)

Schools in Stellenbosch, Paarl, Somerset West, and Cape Town offer full boarding. Bridge House (Franschhoek, ~120 km) and Somerset College (Somerset West, ~90 km) are the nearest high-fee independent options. Cape Town boarding schools are 150+ km away.

**What this costs:** The project's expensive schools research documents fees ranging from roughly R80,000 per year at the lower end of independent boarding to over R450,000 at the top. Most families in Greyton will find this range either comfortable or impossible; it is rarely a borderline decision.

**What this requires:** The child lives away from home for entire terms. Family contact is limited to weekends (if close enough) or school holidays. The child enters a social world very different from the valley. This is a values question as much as a logistics question.

### The child stays home (online/distance schooling)

If the child enrols with an online school, the logistics question becomes a supervision question. Someone needs to be at home (or available) during school hours. The child needs a quiet workspace, a reliable internet connection, and a device.

**What this costs:** Online school fees range from under R500/month (Syllabis, materials-led, minimal live interaction) to R3,000+/month (UCT Online High School, Teneo, with live classes and support advisors). The device and data costs add to this. Fibre or stable Wi-Fi is close to non-negotiable for live-class providers.

**What this requires:** A parent or supervising adult available during school hours, or a child mature enough to manage their own schedule. A space in the home that works as a study environment. The discipline to maintain routine without the social structure of a physical school. A plan for socialisation, sport, and peer interaction outside the platform.

## The compound calculation

Most parents will combine this variable with cost ([[Variable 5: Cost]]) and language ([[Variable 1: Language of Instruction]]) before they get to curriculum or pedagogy questions. The honest version of the calculation is:

- If English-medium is non-negotiable and the family cannot afford boarding, the realistic options are: daily transport to Overberg High School in Caledon, an online school, or Silverwood (if it opens).
- If cost is the binding constraint, Emil Weder is the only option that costs close to nothing in fees and transport.
- If the family can afford independent schooling, the option set opens, but every option except a valley-based school carries a logistics burden.

## Questions to settle before moving on

1. Can your family sustain daily transport to Caledon for five years? In money, time, and reliability?
2. If transport is not sustainable, is your child ready for boarding?
3. If boarding is not right for your child, can you provide the supervision and environment an online school requires?
4. Would you join a lift club or commit to a dedicated transport scheme if enough families participated?
5. How many other Greyton families are making the same calculation? (This number determines whether collective solutions like a dedicated transport service or a new school are viable.)
