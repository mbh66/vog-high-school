Some parents will never reach this variable. Cost, language, and logistics will have already made their decision for them. For families who do have a choice, this is where the question shifts from "which school can we access?" to "which school do we want?"

## Why it matters

Pedagogy is how a school teaches. Values are what a school believes education is for. These shape the daily experience of the child: what the classroom feels like, how teachers relate to learners, how assessment works, what counts as success, and what kind of person the school is trying to produce.

Two schools can follow the same CAPS curriculum, sit the same examination, and produce the same qualification while offering profoundly different experiences. A child at one may spend six hours a day at a desk with a textbook. A child at the other may spend half that time outdoors, in a workshop, or in a group project. Both write matric. The five years in between look nothing alike.

## The approaches available to valley families

### Standard academic instruction

This is what most South African public and independent schools do. The teacher leads classroom instruction, primarily through lectures, textbook work, and structured exercises. Assessment is regular and formal: tests, exams, assignments with rubrics. The rhythm of the year is driven by the assessment calendar.

**Strengths:** Familiar to parents. Produces learners well-prepared for conventional examinations. Transfers easily between schools. Teachers are trained for this model.

**Limitations:** Can be passive for learners who need active or experiential engagement. Content-heavy with limited room for inquiry or creativity. The relationship between what is studied and the learner's own world is often weak.

**Available at:** Emil Weder, Overberg High School, Hoërskool Overberg, most online providers.

### Waldorf/Steiner education

Waldorf schools follow Rudolf Steiner's developmental model, which structures education around three broad phases: early childhood (will-based learning through imitation and play), primary school (feeling-based learning through narrative, art, and rhythm), and high school (thinking-based learning through analysis, inquiry, and independent judgment).

In the high school years, Waldorf education is characterised by:

- **Main Lesson blocks.** Intensive three- to four-week immersions in a single subject, rather than rotating through six or seven subjects daily. A block on botany might run for three weeks of morning sessions, followed by a block on history.
- **Integration of arts and practical work.** Every learner does woodwork, textiles, painting, music, eurythmy (movement), and drama alongside academic subjects. These are not electives; they are structural to the pedagogy.
- **The class teacher model.** In the lower school, one teacher accompanies a class for several years. In the high school, specialist subject teachers take over, but the emphasis on long-term teacher-student relationships persists.
- **Delayed digital technology.** Waldorf schools typically restrict screen-based learning until high school, emphasising direct experience and handwork in earlier years.
- **A matric year (Class 13).** The Waldorf curriculum runs through Class 12. A dedicated Class 13 prepares learners for the NSC examination. This means the child is in school for 13 years, not 12. Some Waldorf schools integrate CAPS content earlier to avoid a full extra year.

**Strengths:** Develops the whole person. Strong arts and practical integration. Learners often report high engagement and a sense of meaning. Matric results at established Waldorf schools are strong (see [[04-curriculum-and-examination-body|Variable 4: Curriculum and Examination Body]]).

**Limitations:** Unfamiliar to most parents. Less transferable mid-stream (a child leaving a Waldorf school at Grade 10 for a conventional CAPS school may face content gaps). Requires trust in a pedagogical philosophy rooted in anthroposophy (Rudolf Steiner's spiritual-philosophical framework). The relationship between Waldorf pedagogy and anthroposophy is a live debate: some schools foreground it, others treat it as historical background. Parents should ask where a given school sits on that spectrum.

**Note on project conventions:** The Valley of Grace project scope excludes anthroposophical vocabulary from external-facing documents. Parents evaluating Silverwood will encounter Waldorf terminology in educational contexts. The decision tool presents the pedagogy on its own terms while respecting this boundary.

**Available at:** Silverwood (if constituted). Stellenbosch Waldorf School (~80 km away). Michael Oak and Constantia Waldorf in Cape Town (~150 km, boarding or relocation required).

### Project-based and experiential learning

Project-based learning (PBL) organises curriculum around sustained projects rather than discrete lessons. The child works on a real-world problem or question over days or weeks, drawing on multiple subjects. Assessment is typically through the project output (a report, a presentation, a built artefact) rather than a separate test.

This is the pedagogical orientation described in the BioHub Founding Brief for Silverwood: "project-based learning as the governing pedagogy, integrated with the BioHub's BioScore and Nature Realm platforms." In this model, a learner's Geography project might involve mapping a section of the valley's watershed using the GoE platform, generating real biodiversity data while meeting CAPS content requirements.

**Strengths:** High engagement. Develops collaboration, problem-solving, and communication. Connects learning to the learner's own environment. Well suited to a valley with the ecological and heritage richness of Ward 2.

**Limitations:** Depends heavily on teacher capacity and project design. A poorly designed project teaches less than a well-structured lesson. Assessment can be opaque to parents used to percentage marks. Requires that the school maintain CAPS alignment within the project structure, which is a design challenge.

**Available at:** Silverwood (proposed). Some online providers incorporate project elements. No conventional school in the immediate area uses PBL as its primary pedagogy.

### Online and self-directed learning

Online schooling is a pedagogy as well as a delivery mechanism. At the self-directed end (Syllabis, Impaq), the child works through materials with minimal live interaction. The parent or a facilitator provides structure and accountability. At the structured end (UCT Online, Teneo, Wingu), live classes, timetables, and support advisors create something closer to a conventional school experience delivered through a screen.

**Strengths:** Flexibility. Location independence. The child can learn at their own pace (within the provider's term structure). Suits children who are self-motivated and disciplined. Can be combined with local activities (sport clubs, art classes, the GoE Nature Realm) to compensate for the absence of a physical school community.

**Limitations:** Social isolation is the most commonly cited risk. The child is at home, alone or with a sibling, for most of the day. Parental involvement is required at a level that varies by provider but is never zero. Practical subjects are harder to deliver online. The experience depends heavily on the home environment: a child with a quiet room, fast internet, and a supportive parent has a different experience from a child sharing a space with younger siblings and intermittent connectivity.

**Available at:** All accredited online providers listed in [[03-school-type-and-registration|Variable 3: School Type and Registration Status]].

### Faith-based education

Emil Weder Secondary operates within the historically Moravian context of Genadendal. Some boarding schools further afield have Christian foundations. For families where faith-based education is a priority, this is a variable worth surfacing. For families where it is not, it can be set aside.

The decision tool does not rank faith-based and secular approaches. It notes that the option exists and that some families weight it.

## Values beyond pedagogy

Pedagogy is a method. Values are broader. Some questions that sit in this space:

**Diversity.** Does the school bring together children from different racial, linguistic, and income backgrounds, or does it select for a narrow band? In the valley, Emil Weder serves primarily Afrikaans-speaking children from Genadendal and surrounding communities. A Greyton-based independent school would draw primarily from English-speaking, relatively affluent families. An online school has no physical peer group at all. Each of these carries a different social formation.

**Connection to place.** Does the school connect the child to the valley they live in, or does it abstract them from it? The Ward 2 Schools Program proposal is built on the premise that a school grounded in the valley's own ecology, heritage, and economy produces a different kind of graduate than one studying generic textbook content. This is an argument, not a fact. Some parents will find it compelling. Others will prioritise examination results regardless of context.

**Preparation for what?** A school that optimises for university entrance is different from one that optimises for local economic participation, which is different from one that optimises for personal development. Most schools claim to do all three. Few do. Parents should be honest about which outcome they care about most and evaluate options against that priority.

## Questions to settle before moving on

1. Does pedagogy matter to you, or are you primarily choosing on language, cost, and logistics?
2. If it matters: do you want a conventional classroom, an experiential or project-based environment, a Waldorf approach, or the flexibility of online learning?
3. How important is it that your child's education is connected to the valley they live in?
4. How do you weigh examination results against broader developmental outcomes?
5. If you are drawn to Waldorf or PBL: have you visited a school that practises it, or are you working from descriptions? The experience of being in a Waldorf classroom is different from reading about one. Stellenbosch Waldorf School is the nearest option for a visit.

## Gaps

- Whether Silverwood's "Waldorf-inspired" means full Waldorf curriculum with CAPS matric or CAPS content with Waldorf methods. **TBV** with founders.
- Whether Silverwood would run a 13-year programme or integrate CAPS content to avoid the extra year.
- Parent experience reports from families whose children attend Waldorf high schools in the Western Cape. These would be the most useful evidence for Greyton parents weighing this approach.
- Whether any Greyton families have direct experience with online high school. First-hand accounts would ground the "limitations" section in local reality.
