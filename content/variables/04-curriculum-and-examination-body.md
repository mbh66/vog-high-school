The curriculum determines what your child learns. The examination body determines how they are assessed at matric and which qualification they receive. These are separate choices, and both matter.

## The curriculum: CAPS or Cambridge

### CAPS (Curriculum and Assessment Policy Statement)

This is South Africa's national curriculum, mandated by the Department of Basic Education. Every public school follows it. Most independent schools follow it (sometimes with enrichment). All online CAPS providers follow it. CAPS specifies the content, sequencing, and assessment weighting for each subject from Grade R to Grade 12.

A child following CAPS in any setting (public school, independent school, or online) covers the same material. The differences between schools lie in how well they teach it, how much they supplement it, and which examination body administers the final assessment.

CAPS has been criticised for being content-heavy and prescriptive, leaving limited room for teacher-led adaptation. Its defenders argue it ensures a national baseline. For parents, the practical point is that CAPS is the path of least friction: every South African university, TVET college, and employer recognises the qualification it leads to without any additional steps.

### Cambridge International

Some independent schools (and some online providers like CambriLearn) offer the Cambridge pathway: IGCSEs at the Grade 10 equivalent, followed by AS and A Levels at the Grade 11-12 equivalent. Cambridge qualifications are internationally recognised and accepted by South African universities, but they are not the NSC. A Cambridge student applying to a South African university must apply separately for a Matriculation Exemption through Universities South Africa (USAf). This is an administrative step, not an academic barrier, but it adds a requirement that CAPS students do not face.

Cambridge suits families planning for international university applications or wanting a curriculum that emphasises depth over breadth. It is more expensive to deliver and examine, and the number of subject choices may be narrower than at a large CAPS school.

### Waldorf curriculum with CAPS compliance

Waldorf (Steiner) schools follow their own developmental curriculum through Classes 1 to 12, then run a dedicated matric year (Class 13 in Waldorf terminology) aligned to the NSC. The Waldorf curriculum integrates arts, crafts, movement, and practical work alongside academic subjects. It is structured around developmental stages rather than the DBE's grade-by-grade content prescription.

At matric, Waldorf learners write the same NSC examination as everyone else, through whichever examination body the school is registered with. South African Waldorf schools report strong NSC results. Constantia Waldorf School achieved a 100% pass rate with 100% bachelor-level endorsements and a 74.9% class average in 2024. Michael Oak (Cape Town) reports 100% pass rates with 99% bachelor endorsements.

The catch is that the Waldorf curriculum through Classes 8 to 12 does not track CAPS content year by year. A child who leaves a Waldorf school mid-stream to enter a CAPS school at Grade 10 may find significant content gaps. The commitment to the Waldorf pathway is most coherent when it runs from Grade 8 through matric.

This is directly relevant to Silverwood, which the BioHub Founding Brief describes as CAPS-compliant and Waldorf-inspired. Parents should clarify what "Waldorf-inspired" means in practice: does Silverwood run the full Waldorf curriculum with a CAPS matric year, or does it follow CAPS content throughout with Waldorf pedagogical methods? The distinction affects transferability, pacing, and the child's options if they need to change schools.

## The examination body: who sets and marks the matric papers

Three bodies administer the NSC in South Africa. All three are accredited by Umalusi, the statutory quality council. The NSC certificate issued under any of the three carries identical legal standing.

### DBE (Department of Basic Education)

Sets and administers the NSC for all public schools and some independent schools. This is the default. If your child attends Emil Weder, Overberg High School, or Hoërskool Overberg, they write the DBE papers.

### IEB (Independent Examinations Board)

Sets its own NSC papers, primarily for independent (private) schools. IEB papers are widely regarded as more demanding in their emphasis on critical thinking and application. Most high-fee independent schools use IEB. Michael Mount Waldorf School in Johannesburg uses IEB. The qualification is identical in status to the DBE NSC.

### SACAI (South African Comprehensive Assessment Institute)

Focuses on distance education and home-schooling. Most accredited online schools (UCT Online High School, Teneo, CambriLearn's CAPS pathway, Wingu Academy) are registered with SACAI. Some of SACAI's examination papers are sourced from the DBE or IEB; others are set independently. The qualification is identical in status to the DBE and IEB NSC.

Umalusi has stated directly that a university cannot differentiate which of the three bodies a learner wrote under when presented with the NSC certificate. Admission decisions are based on the qualification level (Higher Certificate, Diploma, or Bachelor pass) and the subject marks, which convert to the same Admission Point Score (APS) scale regardless of examination body.

## What parents need to know

**Switching mid-stream is costly.** Moving from CAPS to Cambridge (or vice versa) before Grade 10 is manageable. Moving during the FET phase (Grades 10 to 12) is difficult because continuous assessment records (SBAs) may not transfer cleanly between examination bodies. Some bodies require a learner to have completed specific portions of Grades 10 and 11 with them before sitting the Grade 12 examination.

**SBAs are compulsory.** The NSC is not a single exam at the end of Grade 12. School-Based Assessments, completed and moderated throughout Grades 10 to 12, count toward the final mark (typically 25% for most subjects). Every examination body requires them. Online providers must manage SBA submission through their examination body. Parents should ask: how does this school or provider handle SBAs, who moderates them, and what happens if an SBA is flagged for quality concerns?

**Practical subjects need physical arrangements.** Life Sciences and Physical Sciences have practical components. Visual Arts requires portfolio assessment. Technology requires practical projects. Online providers handle this differently: some ship experiment kits, some arrange supervised practical sessions at examination centres, some limit subject offerings to avoid the problem. Parents whose children want practical-heavy subjects should ask specifically how the provider meets those requirements.

**The matric exam is always written in person.** Regardless of whether the child studies online, the final NSC examinations are written at approved, invigilated examination centres. Studying is online; the exam is physical. SACAI operates examination centres nationally. Parents in the valley should confirm where the nearest SACAI or IEB examination centre is and what the logistics of exam-week attendance look like.

## Questions to settle before moving on

1. Is the NSC (South African matric) the right qualification, or is your family considering international pathways?
2. If NSC: does the examination body matter to you, or are you satisfied that DBE, IEB, and SACAI are equivalent?
3. If your child has specific subject interests that require practical work, how does each option you are considering handle those requirements?
4. If you are considering Silverwood: which examination body will it register with, and has it confirmed that registration?
5. Where is the nearest SACAI examination centre for online learners in the valley?
