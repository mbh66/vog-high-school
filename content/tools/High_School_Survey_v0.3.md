# Survey design: draft questions (v0.3)

**Working title:** *An English-medium high school in the Valley of Grace: what would your family need?*


---

## Introduction (shown before the first question)

This survey is for English-speaking families who are thinking about high school for a child starting Grade 8 in 2028 or later, and who are looking at independent or fee-paying options.

It is run by a High School Working Group, made up of parents currently residing in Greyton. It is not run by, or on behalf of, any school or provider. No school has been confirmed, and answering commits you to nothing.

Your answers will help work out how many families have children starting high school each year, what kind of school they would consider, and what they could afford. The High School Decision Tool at hs.valleyofgrace.co.za explains the terms used here.

It takes about 12 minutes. Please send one response per household.

**How your information is used**

- Results are reported only as totals and anonymously. No child or family is named, and small groups are not reported separately if that could identify someone.
- Contact details are not requested. If you would like to receive updates about high school options in the valley, join this WhatsApp group: chat.whatsapp.com/ICo4qtNLb6w88Vtr01yyTS
- You can ask for your response to be deleted at any time by writing to hello@michaelhaupt.com.

## Section 4: What kind of school

Each option below shows a rough annual cost per child. The figures come from the High School Decision Tool (2026 estimates), include transport and other costs, and will change.

- **A. Local learning hub.** Children study with an accredited online school in a shared space in the valley, with a facilitator on site. They write the matric through the online school's examination body. Each family registers for home education with the WCED. About R40,000 to R95,000 a year.
- **B. Independent day school in the valley.** Full-time, daily attendance, a traditional school day. About R55,000 to R100,000 a year, based on comparable Western Cape independent day schools.
- **C. Independent boarding school in the valley.** It takes learners from across South Africa as boarders and offers day places to valley families. Boarding fees at Western Cape independent schools run from about R150,000 to over R400,000 a year. Day-place fees would be lower but are not yet known.
- **D. Grades 8 and 9 in the valley, then a move.** A school in the valley for Grades 8 and 9, followed by a move to another school for Grades 10 to 12. Cost depends on the schools.
- **E. Overberg High School's English stream in Caledon, with shared transport.** A lift club or contracted school bus for the daily 35 km trip. About R19,000 to R51,000 a year with a lift club.
- **F. Family-led hub.** Families arrange their own schooling and qualifications, and share a space in the valley for social and extramural activities. Cost depends on each family's arrangements.

## Notes for the form builder and analysis

- Only Q1 is required. Every other question can be skipped.
- Report results as totals. Do not report any group of fewer than five households separately, and never combine village, child's grade and current school in a way that could point to one family.
- Keep the raw responses and contact details in `~/Quartz/workbench/school/`. They must never go into `content/` or the site repository.
- Q5 gives the number of learners per grade per year, which the Decision Tool lists as its most important missing figure (Variable 9). Q20, Q22 and Q23 give the fee data needed to test whether fee-paying families could cover a new school's running costs.
- Check the village list in Q2 against the W2DF register's village pages. The Decision Tool does not list Boschmanskloof or Middeldorp.
- Cost figures in Section 4 come from the Decision Tool's variable and option pages (September 2026). Update them if those pages change before the survey goes out.
- This survey is English-only by design. A valley-wide survey for all families will follow.
