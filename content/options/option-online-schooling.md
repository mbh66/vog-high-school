# Online / Distance Schooling

A composite profile covering accredited online high school providers. The child studies from home using a digital platform. This is not a single school; it is a category of provision with significant variation between providers.

## Summary assessment

| Variable | Assessment |
|---|---|
| 1. Language | English-medium (most providers). Some offer Afrikaans. |
| 2. Logistics | Child stays home. No commute. Requires supervision and a study environment. |
| 3. School type | Distance education providers registered with SACAI, IEB, or Cambridge. Not WCED-registered schools. |
| 4. Curriculum | CAPS (via SACAI or IEB) or Cambridge. Provider-dependent. |
| 5. Cost | R6,000 to R45,000/year in fees. Add device, data, and supervision costs. |
| 6. Pedagogy | Ranges from self-directed materials to structured live classes. Provider-dependent. |
| 7. Subjects | Core academic subjects covered. Practical subjects limited. |
| 8. Post-school | NSC (SACAI or IEB) or Cambridge. Full university eligibility. |
| 9. Social | No physical peer group. Social life must be constructed separately. |
| 10. Silverwood | Not applicable. |

## Variable-by-variable assessment

### 1. Language of instruction

Most accredited online providers deliver in English. Some (Impaq, Syllabis) also offer Afrikaans. For English-speaking Greyton families, the language requirement is met by the majority of providers.

### 2. Daily logistics

The child stays in the valley. No transport cost. No road risk. No boarding separation.

The logistics shift from travel to supervision. Someone needs to be available during school hours, particularly for younger learners (Grade 8 and 9). The level of supervision required varies by provider:

- **High-structure providers** (UCT Online, Teneo, Wingu): timetabled live classes, regular check-ins with support advisors, structured assessment calendar. The platform imposes routine. Parental role is monitoring, not teaching.
- **Medium-structure providers** (CambriLearn, Curro Online): mix of recorded and live content. More flexibility, more self-discipline required.
- **Low-structure providers** (Impaq, Syllabis): materials-based, self-paced. The parent or facilitator provides most of the daily structure. Suited to highly self-motivated learners or families with a dedicated home-education approach.

The home environment matters. The child needs a quiet workspace, a reliable device (laptop or tablet), and stable internet. Fibre or uncapped Wi-Fi is close to essential for live-class providers. Intermittent connectivity disrupts lessons and assessments.

### 3. School type and registration

Online providers are not registered with the WCED as schools. They are registered with examination bodies (SACAI, IEB, or Cambridge) as distance education providers. The examination body, not the WCED, is the primary quality-assurance mechanism. Umalusi accredits the examination body.

This means: no SGB, no WCED circuit manager, no annual parent budget meeting. The parent's relationship is with the provider (a commercial entity) and the examination body (a regulatory body). If the provider underperforms or closes, the parent's recourse is to the examination body and to consumer protection law.

Parents should confirm before enrolling:
- Is the provider registered with a Umalusi-accredited examination body (SACAI, IEB, or DBE)?
- How long has the provider been operating?
- What are the provider's matric results (pass rate, bachelor-pass rate)?

A provider that is not registered with an accredited examination body cannot issue the NSC. The child's years of study would be unrecognised.

### 4. Curriculum and examination body

Most South African online providers offer CAPS via SACAI. Some offer IEB pathways (Curro Online). CambriLearn offers both CAPS (SACAI) and Cambridge.

The NSC issued through SACAI or IEB carries identical legal standing to the DBE NSC. Universities cannot differentiate between the three on the certificate.

**SBAs:** School-Based Assessments are compulsory. The provider manages SBA submission and moderation through its examination body. Parents should ask how SBAs work: are they submitted online? Are they moderated internally or externally? What happens if an SBA is flagged?

**Final examinations:** Written in person at an invigilated examination centre. SACAI operates centres nationally. The nearest centre to Greyton has not been confirmed (TBV with SACAI). The child will need to travel to the centre for the exam period (typically October/November for Grade 12).

### 5. Cost

| Provider | Approximate annual fee | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Syllabis | Under R6,000 | Materials-led, minimal live interaction |
| Impaq | R8,000 to R15,000 | Parent-facilitated, CAPS materials |
| CambriLearn (CAPS) | R15,000 to R40,000 | Recorded + live, varies by package |
| Wingu Academy | R20,000 to R45,000 | Live online teaching |
| UCT Online High School | ~R36,000 | Live classes, support advisors |
| Teneo | ~R36,000 | AI-assisted, live classes |
| Curro Online | Varies | IEB pathway available |

**Additional costs:**
- Device: R5,000 to R15,000 (once-off, replaced every 3 to 4 years)
- Internet: R6,000 to R18,000/year
- SACAI examination registration: R2,000 to R5,000/year (per-subject fees)

**Total estimated annual cost (parent supervises, mid-range provider):** R25,000 to R55,000.

**Five-year cost:** R125,000 to R275,000.

The cost is comparable to the Overberg High School lift-club option. The trade-off is supervision time instead of driving time.

### 6. Pedagogy and values

This varies so widely between providers that a single assessment is misleading.

At one end: Syllabis and Impaq are essentially textbook-based home education with examination registration. The pedagogy is whatever the parent or facilitator brings to it.

At the other end: UCT Online and Teneo offer timetabled live classes with trained teachers, AI-assisted learning tools, support advisors, and structured assessment. The experience approximates a conventional school delivered through a screen.

In between: CambriLearn and Wingu offer recorded lessons with periodic live interaction, discussion forums, and project work.

No online provider connects the curriculum to the valley's ecology or heritage. The content is generic CAPS (or Cambridge). Place-based learning would need to be added through extracurricular activities: GoE BioQuest, citizen science, heritage walks, or a locally organised enrichment programme.

### 7. Subject availability

Most providers offer: English, Afrikaans, Mathematics, Mathematical Literacy, Life Orientation, Physical Sciences, Life Sciences, Geography, History, Accounting, Business Studies, Economics, CAT (Computer Applications Technology).

Some add: IT, Visual Arts, Design, Tourism.

Few or none offer: Technology subjects (Civil, Electrical, Mechanical), Hospitality Studies, Agricultural Sciences, Music, Dramatic Arts, Dance Studies. These require physical delivery that online platforms cannot replicate.

**Practical subject components:** Physical Sciences and Life Sciences have practical requirements. Some providers ship experiment kits. Some arrange supervised practical sessions at examination centres. Some limit their offering to avoid the problem. Parents choosing science subjects should confirm how practicals are handled.

### 8. Post-school pathway

The NSC (SACAI or IEB) qualifies for all South African post-school pathways. Universities, TVET colleges, and employers recognise it without qualification.

UCT Online High School received 19,000+ applications for its 2026 intake, indicating mainstream acceptance of online schooling at the secondary level. The stigma that once attached to distance education is fading.

The child's university or TVET application is assessed on the qualification and the marks, not the delivery mechanism.

### 9. Social and community considerations

This is the critical weakness of online schooling. The child has no physical school community. No playground. No corridor conversations. No sports teams (unless arranged separately). No school plays or assemblies.

Social life must be actively constructed:
- Local sport clubs (cricket, rugby, swimming, hiking, mountain biking in Greyton)
- GoE BioQuest cycles (April and October)
- Arts and music activities in the valley
- Play dates and peer groups organised by parents
- A microschool arrangement (see [[Option Profile: Microschool]]) that adds a physical peer component

For a self-motivated, socially resilient child with an active family and a rich out-of-school life, this can work well. For a child who needs the social scaffold of a school, the absence is keenly felt.

Parents should ask honestly: can my family provide the social environment this child needs if school does not?

### 10. Silverwood-specific

Not applicable. Online schooling is the alternative to Silverwood, not a version of it. If Silverwood opens and the family prefers a physical school, online schooling drops off the list. If Silverwood does not open, online schooling may be the fallback.

## Best fit

Online schooling suits families where: English-medium instruction is required; the family cannot or does not want to commute to Caledon; the child is self-motivated and disciplined (or the family can provide structure); a parent or facilitator is available during school hours; the home environment supports focused study (space, connectivity, quiet); the family can actively construct a social life for the child outside the platform.

It is a particularly strong option for: families already practising home education; families with flexible work arrangements; older learners (Grade 10+) who have established study habits; families combining online school with a strong local activity programme (sport, GoE, arts).

It is a poor fit where: the child needs the social structure of a physical school; no parent or adult is available for supervision; the home environment is not conducive to study; the child needs practical subjects that online providers cannot deliver; the family wants the child's education grounded in the valley's place-specific context.

## Key unknowns

- Nearest SACAI examination centre to Greyton. **TBV** with SACAI.
- Which providers offer the specific subjects your child wants. **Check each provider's prospectus directly.**
- Matric results for each provider (pass rate, bachelor-pass rate, average marks by subject). Some providers publish this; others do not. **TBV** per provider.
- Fibre or high-speed internet availability in your specific location in Greyton or Genadendal. Some areas have fibre; some do not.
- Whether any Greyton families are currently using online high school. First-hand experience reports would be the most useful input for other parents.
