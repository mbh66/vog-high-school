**This profile is based entirely on project documents (MS). No independent corroboration of Silverwood's plans, registration, staffing, or fee structure has been obtained. Parents should treat every claim here as TBV until confirmed directly with the founders.**

## Variable-by-variable assessment

### 1. Language of instruction

English-medium. This is stated in the project documents. If confirmed, Silverwood would be the only English-medium high school in the valley.

### 2. Daily logistics

The BioHub Founding Brief places Silverwood at Genadendal, within the Learning Hub. The Greyton Commonage Research Report documents a contested R100 sale of ~8 ha on Erf 595 to "a Greyton private school." Whether these refer to the same proposal is unclear. The location is unresolved.

If located at Genadendal (~7 km from Greyton): same logistics as Emil Weder. Short drive or bicycle.

If located on Erf 595 in Greyton: walking distance for Greyton children. Slightly further for Genadendal families.

Either way, transport cost would be minimal. The child sleeps at home.

### 3. School type and registration

Silverwood would be a registered independent school under SASA Section 46. Registration requires a completed application, a school constitution, curriculum documentation, compliant premises, and registration with an examination body.

**As of September 2026, none of these have been confirmed publicly.** The Ward 2 Schools Program proposal (July 2026) refers to Silverwood's participation "once its constitution is confirmed," indicating that the constitution was not finalised at the time of writing.

A school that is not registered with the WCED cannot legally operate. A child enrolled in an unregistered school risks their year being unrecognised. Parents should not enrol until registration is confirmed.

### 4. Curriculum and examination body

"CAPS-compliant, Waldorf-inspired" is the description in the project documents. This formulation is ambiguous (see [[Variable 6: Pedagogy and Values]]):

**Interpretation A:** Full Waldorf curriculum through Class 12, with a Class 13 matric year following CAPS. This is what established Waldorf schools in South Africa do (Michael Oak, Constantia Waldorf, Roseway Waldorf). It means a 13-year programme.

**Interpretation B:** CAPS content throughout, delivered using Waldorf teaching methods and integrated with project-based learning. This is a 12-year programme that looks different from conventional CAPS delivery but covers the same content on the same schedule.

The distinction matters for transferability (can the child move to another school mid-stream?), for pacing, and for the examination body alignment. Parents need to know which interpretation applies.

The examination body (IEB, SACAI, or DBE) has not been confirmed.

### 5. Cost

No fee schedule has been published. Comparable Waldorf schools in the Western Cape provide a reference range:

| School | Annual tuition |
|---|---|
| Stellenbosch Waldorf | R52,000 to R89,000 |
| Constantia Waldorf | R60,000 to R90,000 |
| Michael Oak | R70,000 to R95,000 |

If Silverwood fees land in a comparable range: R50,000 to R100,000/year, plus minimal transport (in-valley). Five-year cost: R275,000 to R500,000 (with annual increases).

Whether Silverwood would offer bursaries, a sliding-scale fee, or means-tested financial aid is unknown. The valley's income diversity (affluent Greyton retirees alongside low-income Genadendal and Farm 39 families) makes affordability design a structural question. A school that charges R80,000/year serves one segment of the valley. A school that offers sliding-scale access serves a different proposition entirely.

### 6. Pedagogy and values

This is Silverwood's distinctive proposition. The BioHub Founding Brief describes:

- **Waldorf-inspired pedagogy:** Main Lesson blocks, arts integration, developmental approach, emphasis on the whole child.
- **Project-based learning as the governing pedagogy:** curriculum organised around sustained projects rather than discrete lessons.
- **Integration with the BioHub's BioScore and Nature Realm platforms:** learner fieldwork generates real biodiversity and heritage data for the GoE Valley of Grace Realm.
- **Place-based education:** Geography is the Riviersonderend watershed. History is the Khoi sanctuary and Moravian mission. Life Sciences is fynbos ecology. Tourism is the valley's own destination economy.

If delivered as described, this would be a distinctive educational experience available nowhere else in the region. The child's learning would be grounded in the valley they live in, connected to real ecological and economic activity, and assessed through both project outputs and formal CAPS examinations.

The risk is execution. This pedagogy requires teachers trained in both Waldorf methods and CAPS compliance, a curriculum design that integrates project work with content coverage, and an examination body arrangement that accepts project-based SBAs. Each of these is achievable in principle. Whether Silverwood has the team, the design, and the institutional relationships to deliver them is the question.

### 7. Subject availability

Unknown. A start-up school with a small first cohort will offer a limited menu. The BioHub Founding Brief's emphasis on Life Sciences, Geography, History, Tourism, and Mathematics suggests these five subjects are priorities. Whether Physical Sciences, Accounting, Business Studies, IT, or arts subjects would be offered from the outset is unclear.

Parents whose children need specific electives should confirm availability before committing. If Silverwood cannot offer Physical Sciences, for example, it closes the door to medicine and engineering pathways regardless of how strong its other offerings are.

### 8. Post-school pathway

If Silverwood achieves WCED registration and examination body accreditation, its NSC will carry the same standing as any other school's. The pathway question then becomes: does the school prepare learners well enough to achieve the marks their post-school ambitions require?

Established Waldorf schools produce strong matric results (100% pass rates, high bachelor-pass rates at Constantia Waldorf, Michael Oak). A new school with untested teachers and an unproven curriculum design does not yet have a track record.

The first matric cohort would sit their exams in 2032 (if Silverwood opens Grade 8 in 2028). Parents enrolling for 2028 are trusting that the school will be accredited, staffed, and functioning at matric level five years later.

The GoE integration may produce portfolio elements (citizen science contributions, heritage evidence work, BioScore records) that strengthen university or international applications. Whether these are formally recognised by any institution is TBV.

### 9. Social and community considerations

If Silverwood opens with enough Greyton families enrolled, the peer group stays local. Children walk to school with neighbours. Friendships form in the valley. After-school activities happen in Greyton and Genadendal. The child's social centre of gravity remains where they live.

This is the strongest social argument for Silverwood and the strongest argument for a local school of any kind.

The social composition depends on the fee structure. A high-fee school draws a narrow demographic. A school with bursaries and sliding-scale fees draws a wider one. The founders' decisions about affordability determine whether Silverwood serves the whole valley or only its wealthier residents.

### 10. Silverwood-specific questions

The full checklist of 18 questions is in [[Variable 10: Silverwood-Specific Questions]]. The three highest-priority questions for the planning session:

1. **Is the school registered with the WCED, or when will it be?** Without registration, nothing else matters.
2. **What is the fee, and is there a sliding-scale or bursary provision?** This determines who can attend.
3. **How many families have committed to enrol for 2028?** This determines whether the school is viable.

## Best fit

Silverwood suits families where: English-medium, in-valley schooling is the priority; the family is drawn to Waldorf pedagogy and/or project-based learning; the family values education grounded in the valley's ecology and heritage; the family can afford independent school fees (or bursaries are available); the family is comfortable enrolling in a school that does not yet have a track record.

It is a poor fit where: the family needs certainty about school quality, staffing, and matric outcomes before enrolling; the child needs subjects Silverwood may not offer; the family cannot afford independent school fees and no financial aid is available; the family is unwilling to accept the risk inherent in a start-up school.

## Key unknowns

All 18 questions listed in [[Variable 10: Silverwood-Specific Questions]] remain TBV. The most consequential are WCED registration status, examination body, fee structure, minimum viable enrolment, and the number of committed families.
