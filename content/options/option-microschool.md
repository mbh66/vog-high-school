# Microschool / Learning Pod

A group of families pooling resources to educate their children together in the valley, using an accredited online curriculum supplemented by a local facilitator and shared physical space. This is not a registered school. It is a structured form of collective home education.

## Summary assessment

| Variable | Assessment |
|---|---|
| 1. Language | English (determined by the online provider chosen). |
| 2. Logistics | In-valley. Child stays local. Minimal transport. |
| 3. School type | Home education (each family registered with WCED) + online provider. Not a school. |
| 4. Curriculum | CAPS via SACAI (or Cambridge, depending on provider). |
| 5. Cost | R45,000 to R80,000/year per child (provider + shared facilitator + venue). |
| 6. Pedagogy | Determined by provider + facilitator. Highly customisable. |
| 7. Subjects | Constrained by provider's subject list + facilitator's competencies. |
| 8. Post-school | NSC (SACAI or IEB). Full eligibility. |
| 9. Social | Small, close peer group in the valley. Not a school community. |
| 10. Silverwood | Functions as an alternative if Silverwood does not open, or a bridge until it does. |

## What a microschool is

A microschool is not a school in the legal sense. It is a group of families, each individually registered for home education with the WCED, who share a physical learning space, a facilitator, and an online curriculum provider.

The legal structure: each parent is the registered home educator. The online provider delivers the curriculum and manages examination registration (typically through SACAI). The facilitator provides daily supervision, tutoring, and the human interaction the online platform cannot. The shared space provides a classroom-like environment that the home often cannot.

The result: the child gets a recognised NSC qualification, a physical peer group, daily structure, adult supervision, and access to a facilitator, without requiring WCED school registration, a full teaching staff, or a building that meets school premises standards.

## Variable-by-variable assessment

### 1. Language of instruction

Determined by the online provider. Most offer English. Some offer Afrikaans. The facilitator can work in whichever language the group needs.

### 2. Daily logistics

The child goes to a local space (a community hall, a converted room, a rented house, a space at the BioHub) each school day. The commute is within the valley. No mountain pass. No 35 km drive.

If the space is in Greyton, Greyton children walk. Genadendal children need a short drive (~7 km) or arranged transport. If the space is at Genadendal (perhaps within the BioHub), the positions reverse.

### 3. School type and registration

Each family registers independently for home education with the WCED. The WCED requires that the parent submit an application, specify the curriculum, and agree to potential inspections (which are infrequent but legally possible).

The microschool itself is not registered as a school. It does not need to be. The legal schooling arrangement runs through the individual home-education registration and the accredited online provider. The shared space and the facilitator are support structures.

This means: no SGB, no school fees (in the legal sense; the facilitator and venue are costs shared between families), and no WCED oversight of the microschool as an institution. Accountability runs through the examination body (for the qualification) and the WCED (for the home-education registration).

**Legal risk:** If the WCED interprets the microschool as an unregistered school operating under the guise of home education, it could take action. The risk is low if each family holds a valid home-education registration and the arrangement is genuinely parent-led, but it is not zero. Parents should ensure their individual registrations are current and compliant.

### 4. Curriculum and examination body

Determined by the online provider. CAPS via SACAI is the most common pathway. The child writes the same NSC as any other SACAI-registered learner.

The facilitator supplements the curriculum with in-person teaching, discussion, and practical work. If the facilitator is a qualified science teacher, for example, supervised laboratory practicals become possible. This addresses one of the weaknesses of pure online schooling.

### 5. Cost

The cost has three components:

| Component | Low estimate | High estimate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Online provider fees | R15,000/year | R40,000/year | Per child. Provider-dependent. |
| Shared facilitator | R12,000/year | R25,000/year | Per child. Assumes R10,000-R15,000/month facilitator cost shared among 6-8 families. |
| Venue | R3,000/year | R10,000/year | Per child. Depends on whether space is donated, rented, or purpose-fitted. |
| Device and data | R8,000/year | R15,000/year | Per child. Device amortised over 3-4 years. |
| SACAI examination fees | R2,000/year | R5,000/year | Per child. Per-subject registration. |
| **Total per child** | **R40,000/year** | **R95,000/year** | |

**Five-year cost:** R200,000 to R475,000 per child.

The cost per child drops as more families join. Six families sharing a facilitator pay roughly half per child compared to three families. The economics improve with scale, up to a natural ceiling of about 12 to 15 learners per facilitator.

### 6. Pedagogy and values

This is the most customisable option on the list. The online provider delivers the core curriculum. Everything the facilitator adds is chosen by the parent group: nature-based learning using GoE, heritage research, practical skills, art, music, sport, community service.

A microschool in the valley could integrate the Ward 2 Schools Program's place-based modules (ecology, heritage, local economy) alongside the online curriculum. It could use the BioHub as a learning venue. It could incorporate BioQuest cycles as fieldwork.

The risk is the opposite of a school's risk: with full parental control comes the full burden of design. A microschool is only as good as its facilitator, its curriculum choices, and the commitment of its parent group. Without at least one parent who drives the design and holds the group together, it drifts.

### 7. Subject availability

Constrained by the online provider's subject list (see [[Option Profile: Online Schooling]]) plus whatever the facilitator can teach. If the facilitator is qualified in Physical Sciences, the group gains supervised practicals. If not, practical subjects remain limited to the online provider's arrangements.

A group that deliberately recruits a facilitator with science qualifications expands its subject range. A group that hires a generalist has a narrower academic offering but may gain in pastoral care and general support.

### 8. Post-school pathway

NSC via SACAI (or IEB, depending on provider). Full eligibility for university, TVET, and employment. Same as online schooling.

The microschool can add portfolio elements (GoE contributions, heritage research, community projects) that strengthen post-school applications, particularly for international universities that value demonstrated community engagement.

### 9. Social and community considerations

The peer group is small (6 to 12 children) and local. These children see each other every day, in person, in the valley. Friendships form in the microschool and extend into village life. The social experience is intimate, not institutional.

The limitation is scale. A group of eight is not a school. There is no inter-house sports day, no school play with a cast of forty, no corridor culture. The social world is narrower. Whether this is a strength (close bonds, safety, pastoral attention) or a weakness (limited social exposure, no anonymity, clique risks in a small group) depends on the child and the group dynamics.

For socialisation beyond the microschool, the same compensating strategies apply as for online schooling: local sport clubs, GoE activities, arts programmes, and deliberate peer-group formation.

### 10. Silverwood-specific

A microschool functions as a bridge or an alternative to Silverwood:

- **If Silverwood opens in 2028:** families who prefer a formal school can enrol. The microschool dissolves or continues for families who prefer the model.
- **If Silverwood does not open in 2028:** the microschool provides an in-valley, English-medium, peer-supported option that requires no school registration, no building permit, and no minimum enrolment threshold.
- **If Silverwood opens later (2029, 2030):** the microschool can serve as a one- or two-year holding arrangement, with children transitring to Silverwood once it is operational.

The microschool is the lowest-commitment collective solution. It can start with three families and a facilitator. It does not need a building, a constitution, WCED registration, or R2 million in start-up capital. It needs a living room, a good internet connection, and a competent adult.

## Best fit

A microschool suits families where: English-medium, in-valley education is the priority; the family values a small, close peer group; the family wants pedagogical flexibility and local customisation; the budget can sustain R40,000 to R95,000/year; at least 4 to 6 families are willing to commit; the parent group includes at least one person with the capacity to organise and sustain the arrangement; Silverwood is not yet available or not preferred.

It is a poor fit where: the family wants the institutional security of a registered school; the child needs a large peer group; no parent in the group has the organisational capacity to hold it together; the family is uncomfortable with the legal ambiguity of a microschool operating under home-education registrations.

## Key unknowns

- Whether 4 to 8 Greyton families would commit to a microschool arrangement for 2028. **TBV** through the planning session.
- Availability of a suitable facilitator in the valley. **TBV** locally. The facilitator's qualifications and personality determine the quality of the experience.
- Availability of a suitable venue. **TBV**. The BioHub (if operational), a community hall, a church facility, or a private home could work.
- The WCED's current stance on microschools operating under home-education registrations. **TBV** with the WCED district office.
- Whether any online provider offers discounted group rates for microschool arrangements. Some do. **TBV** per provider.
