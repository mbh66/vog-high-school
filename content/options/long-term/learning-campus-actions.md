What has to happen, in what order, for the Valley of Grace Learning Campus to move from concept to operating institution. Each action states what needs doing, who can do it, what it depends on, and what happens next.

The actions are grouped by phase (see [[learning-campus|main profile]] for the phasing rationale). Within each phase they are roughly ordered by dependency: things that gate other things come first. Some actions span phases. Calendar dates assume a November 2026 start; they shift if the start slips.

This is a working list for planning purposes. It is not a project plan agreed with the community. Several of these actions (particularly those involving governance, site, and Genadendal engagement) cannot be taken unilaterally. The body handling the coordination is the *Ward 2 Development Forum: Business and Community (W2DF)*, convened on 16 September, 2026.


## Phase 0: Groundwork (November 2026 to mid-2027)

Phase 0 is about securing the people, permissions, and information needed before any programme launches. Nothing in this phase costs significant money. Everything in this phase costs time and relationships.

### Governance and community engagement

**Action 0.1: Appoint a W2DF education/skills champion.**
The November 2026 W2DF meeting is scheduled to assign champions to each priority area. The education and skills champion becomes the point person for the learning campus concept within the forum. This should not be a Greyton-only initiative; a Genadendal co-champion is essential for legitimacy.
- Depends on: November 2026 W2BF meeting taking place.
- Opens: a mandate to convene the working group (0.2).

**Action 0.2: Convene a learning campus working group.**
A subset of the W2BF focused specifically on the campus concept. Must include Genadendal representation (Transformasie Komitee nominee at minimum), a commercial farming representative, and ideally someone with education sector experience. The working group's first task is to agree on scope: are we exploring this, or are we committing to build it?
Depends on: 0.1, and the protocol commitment made at the inaugural W2BF (share proposals with the Transformasie Komitee in writing before seeking endorsement).
Opens: a legitimate body to commission the feasibility study (0.5), engage partners (0.6 to 0.9), and make site recommendations (0.4).

**Action 0.3: Formal engagement with the Transformasie Komitee.**
Present the learning campus concept to the Komitee in writing. Not for endorsement; for input. The Komitee's 8-sectorial plan should inform what the campus offers, not the other way round. Ask: does this concept serve priorities the Komitee has already identified? What would need to change for it to have the Komitee's support?
Depends on: 0.1. The TGF proposal sharing (W2BF Action Item 8) should happen first as a trust-building step.
Opens: clarity on whether the Genadendal community sees this as useful. Without this, the project should not proceed.

**Action 0.4: Site identification.**
The working group identifies candidate sites. Three scenarios outlined in the working document: Genadendal (closest to population, complicated by TRANCRAA), Greyton (closer to English-speaking families, risks protocol failure), or between the two. The Hester Dorothea Conference Centre in Genadendal is a possible interim venue for Phase 1 programmes while a permanent site is resolved.
Depends on: 0.2 and 0.3 (the site question is inseparable from the governance question).
Opens: facility planning, lease or build decisions, and the WCED school registration application (which requires a premises description).

### Feasibility and planning

**Action 0.5: Commission or assemble a feasibility study.**
The study must answer: How many high school learners? (Survey valley families with children aged 10 to 14.) How many adults for which skills programmes? (Use the ward-level skills needs list from W2BF Action Item 5.) How many tourists and at what price point? What is the minimum viable combined revenue to sustain a facility with three staff and shared infrastructure? What are the five-year operating costs?
Depends on: 0.2 (the working group commissions or conducts it). The skills needs list from W2BF Action Item 5 (Guy, with Stalin Govender facilitating) feeds directly into demand estimates.
Opens: a go/no-go decision for Phase 1. Without demand data, everything else is speculation.

**Action 0.6: Map accreditation timelines.**
QCTO SDP accreditation, CATHSSETA training provider accreditation, WCED school registration, and CET satellite establishment each have their own application process and lead time. These can run in parallel but need to be mapped against the phasing plan so that applications are submitted in time. The feasibility study should include this mapping.
Depends on: nothing (desk research; can begin immediately).
Opens: a realistic calendar for Phase 1 and Phase 2 launches.

### Partnership approaches

**Action 0.7: Approach the Western Cape CET College about a Genadendal satellite.**
The Overberg Community Learning Centre (under the WC CET College) already operates satellites at Bredasdorp, Riviersonderend, and Swellendam. Genadendal does not have one. The approach asks: would the CET College host a satellite in Genadendal if a suitable venue and demonstrated community need exist? This brings state-funded adult basic education (AET Levels 1 to 4, GETC) at essentially no cost to the campus.
Depends on: 0.4 (a venue to offer) and 0.3 (community support).
Opens: the adult education pipeline. A community member who completes GETC through the CET satellite becomes eligible for Elsenburg's Livestock Farmer qualification (NQF 3, requires Grade 9 equivalent) and, with matric, for the tourist guide qualification.
Contact: Western Cape CET College, Overberg CLC.

**Action 0.8: Approach Elsenburg about satellite delivery and host farm coordination.**
Two asks. First: would Elsenburg support satellite delivery of short courses or practical blocks closer to the valley? (They already run decentralised training at Bredasdorp, approximately 60 km from Genadendal.) Second: would Elsenburg work with the W2BF to coordinate host farm placements for valley applicants, so the application bottleneck (securing a host farm letter) is handled collectively rather than individually?
Depends on: 0.2 (the working group provides the institutional partner for Elsenburg to deal with) and W2BF Action Item 5 (the skills needs list identifies how many learners and which qualifications).
Opens: agricultural training without waiting for the campus to be built. If valley commercial farmers register as host farms and Elsenburg accepts applicants, this can operate from the 2028 intake (applications 1 April to 30 June 2027).
Contact: DOA.Study@westerncape.gov.za / +27 21 808 5451.

**Action 0.9: Approach CATHSSETA about training provider accreditation.**
Accreditation as a training provider allows the campus to deliver tourist guide qualifications and claim SETA learnership funding. The application requires qualified facilitators (Paul Adendorf holds both cultural and adventure guide accreditation), a venue, and an assessment system. The Western Cape Government also has a formal application process for tourism training provider accreditation.
Depends on: 0.4 (a venue) and a facilitator willing to commit. Paul Adendorf's interest should be confirmed directly.
Opens: funded tourist guide training in the valley. CATHSSETA learnership funding covers training costs and provides a learner stipend.
Contact: info@cathsseta.org.za; WCG DEDAT tourism training accreditation.

**Action 0.10: Meet with Silverwood proponents.**
The Decision Tool profiles Silverwood as a proposed independent school. The campus model is broader (school plus skills centre plus BLC), but there may be overlap in constituency and intent. A direct conversation establishes whether the two initiatives compete, complement, or merge.
Depends on: nothing. Can happen at any time.
Opens: clarity on whether the high school component is being duplicated, and whether resources and energy should be pooled.

**Action 0.11: Engage Emil Weder SGB.**
Ask whether Emil Weder learners could access skills centre and BLC programmes as supplementary offerings. If yes, the campus serves as a ward-wide resource, not a rival to the existing school. This also demonstrates that the campus strengthens what exists rather than fragmenting it.
Depends on: 0.2 (the working group as the interlocutor, not an individual).
Opens: a broader constituency for the skills centre from day one, and a cooperative rather than competitive relationship with the only high school currently in the valley.

### Legal and financial

**Action 0.12: Register the NPC.**
Register a Non-Profit Company with CIPC. The NPC is the legal vehicle for the campus: it will hold the school registration, the SDP accreditation, the CET satellite agreement, and any leases or property. Draft the Memorandum of Incorporation with objects broad enough to cover education, training, research, tourism, and community development.
Depends on: 0.2 (the working group agrees on founding members and governance structure). The board composition question (who sits on it, how Genadendal and Greyton are represented) must be settled before incorporation.
Opens: a legal entity that can open a bank account, sign agreements, apply for accreditation, receive grant funding, and register as a PBO with SARS for Section 18A tax-deductible donations.
Timeline: 2 to 3 months from submission.

**Action 0.13: Apply for PBO and NPO registration.**
Register the NPC as a Non-Profit Organisation with the Department of Social Development and as a Public Benefit Organisation with SARS. PBO status unlocks Section 18A tax-deductible donations, which matters for donor and CSI funding.
Depends on: 0.12.
Opens: the ability to receive tax-deductible donations and to access grant funding that requires NPO/PBO status.

**Action 0.14: Secure seed funding for Phase 1.**
Phase 1 programmes (CET satellite, first skills programmes, BLC community education) have low capital requirements but need operational funding: a venue lease or agreement, materials, facilitator stipends where SETA funding has not yet come through, and project coordination. Sources to approach: the TGF grant (if awarded; includes heritage guide training), Rotary (Keith Radmore offered Rotary involvement), SEFA (Stalin Govender identified SEFA support), and any conservation or heritage grants aligned with the BLC's work.
Depends on: 0.5 (feasibility study gives funders confidence), 0.12 (a legal entity to receive funds).
Opens: Phase 1 launch.


## Phase 1: Skills centre and CET satellite (mid-2027 to end of 2028)

Phase 1 proves the model works before the school component is added. It generates revenue, builds a track record, and demonstrates demand.

**Action 1.1: Launch the CET satellite.**
If the WC CET College agrees (Action 0.7), open the satellite with AET Levels 1 to 4 and GETC programmes. State-funded educators are provided by the CET College. The campus provides the venue and local coordination. Target: adult community members who did not complete schooling.
Depends on: 0.7 (CET College agreement), 0.4 (venue), 0.14 (operational funding for venue and coordination).

**Action 1.2: Launch the first SETA-funded skills programmes.**
Begin with the programmes that have the clearest demand and the most accessible accreditation:
- Tourist guide training (CATHSSETA), using Paul Adendorf as facilitator if he is willing. First cohort could be as few as 8 to 10 learners.
- Agricultural occupational qualifications, coordinated with Elsenburg (Action 0.8). The campus handles applications, host farm matching, and pastoral support; Elsenburg delivers the programme.
- Basic business skills (short course format, potentially SEFA-funded).
Depends on: 0.9 (CATHSSETA accreditation), 0.8 (Elsenburg partnership), 0.14 (seed funding), 0.4 (venue).

**Action 1.3: Begin BLC community education programmes.**
Heritage walks, ecological literacy workshops, food sovereignty sessions, indigenous knowledge documentation. These are not accredited programmes; they build community engagement, foot traffic, and a constituency for the broader campus. They also test the tourism product: can the BLC attract visitors?
Depends on: 0.4 (a base of operations), 0.14 (modest funding for materials and facilitators).

**Action 1.4: Coordinate Elsenburg applications for the 2028 intake.**
Applications open 1 April 2027, close 30 June 2027. The campus (or the working group, if the NPC is not yet operational) acts as the coordination point: identifying eligible community members, matching them with host farms (using the commercial farmer relationships from the W2BF), assisting with applications, and tracking outcomes.
Depends on: 0.8 (Elsenburg partnership confirmed), W2BF Action Item 5 (skills needs list identifies demand), commercial farmers agreeing to register as host farms.
Opens: the first valley residents entering Elsenburg's programme, which is a visible result the community can point to.

**Action 1.5: Track and document outcomes.**
Record enrolment, completion rates, employment outcomes, community participation, and revenue for every programme. This data feeds the feasibility case for Phase 2 (the school) and supports future funding applications. It also allows the working group to drop programmes that do not attract demand and add ones that do.
Depends on: 1.1 to 1.3 being operational.

**Action 1.6: Build the staffing model.**
Identify people who can work across components. A Tourism teacher who also facilitates CATHSSETA adult training. An Agriculture teacher who coordinates the Elsenburg programme. A conservation specialist who runs BLC field programmes and could teach Life Sciences. These are not standard profiles; they need to be identified and, in some cases, developed during Phase 1 before the school opens.
Depends on: 1.2 and 1.3 (the skills programmes and BLC reveal who the right people are).


## Phase 2: High school registration and opening (2028 to 2029)

Phase 2 adds the school component. This is the most regulated, most expensive, and most consequential step.

**Action 2.1: Apply to register the independent school with the WCED.**
The application under SASA Section 46 requires: a constitution, a description of the curriculum (CAPS or equivalent), details of the premises, information about the governing body, and evidence of financial viability. The skills centre's track record from Phase 1 strengthens the financial viability case. Apply 12 to 18 months before intended opening.
Depends on: 0.12 (NPC exists), 0.4 (site secured), 0.5/1.5 (demand data and track record), the working group/board deciding to proceed.
Opens: the school, if approved.

**Action 2.2: Choose an examination body.**
SACAI or IEB. SACAI has lower administrative burden and is used by many smaller independent schools and online providers. IEB has higher prestige and cost, and a better fit if the school aims at a university-track reputation. The choice affects curriculum planning, assessment design, and external perception.
Depends on: 2.1 (the application process requires this decision).

**Action 2.3: Recruit the founding principal and first teachers.**
The founding principal sets the culture. For this model, the principal must understand independent school administration, bioregional education, and community development. The first teachers (two or three for a Grade 8-only opening) must be comfortable teaching across the school and skills centre components.
Depends on: 2.1 (WCED registration in progress or approved), 0.14/1.5 (funding and revenue sufficient to offer salaries).

**Action 2.4: Enrol the first Grade 8 cohort.**
Target: 15 to 20 learners for the first year. Growing by one year group annually, reaching Grade 12 in 2032 or 2033. Enrolment marketing draws on the Decision Tool's family network, the W2BF constituency, and the track record established in Phase 1.
Depends on: 2.1 (school registered), 2.3 (staff hired), 0.4 (premises ready).

**Action 2.5: Integrate high school learners into skills centre programmes.**
From the first year, Grade 8 learners participate in age-appropriate BLC programmes (field trips, heritage walks, ecological literacy). From Grade 10, learners access skills centre programmes as elective and practical components: Tourism learners join guide training; Agricultural Sciences learners join Elsenburg practical blocks. This integration is the model's core innovation and must be designed, not improvised.
Depends on: 1.2 (skills programmes operational), 2.4 (learners enrolled).


## Phase 3: Maturation (2029 to 2033)

**Action 3.1: Grow the school to five year groups.**
Add one grade per year. By 2032 or 2033, the school has Grades 8 to 12 and writes its first matric examination. Each new year group increases fee revenue and strengthens the school's viability.

**Action 3.2: Expand skills centre programmes based on demand.**
Phase 1 data (Action 1.5) shows which programmes attract demand and which do not. Add programmes, drop others, and adjust. Possible additions: hospitality skills (CATHSSETA), alien vegetation clearing and ecological restoration (EPWP or CapeNature partnership), digital skills (MICT SETA).

**Action 3.3: Develop the BLC as a recognised institution.**
By Phase 3, the BLC should be hosting research (2038 Genadendal tricentenary, leopard corridor, bioprecipitation), receiving international bioregional visitors, and running regular tourism programming. This requires building relationships with universities, conservation bodies, and the international bioregional network during Phases 1 and 2.

**Action 3.4: First matric results.**
The first cohort writes matric in 2032 or 2033. Results will define the school's reputation. The examination body choice (Action 2.2), staffing quality (Action 2.3), and integration with skills programmes (Action 2.5) all feed into this moment.


## Actions that span all phases

**Ongoing: Maintain the W2BF relationship.**
The campus concept arose from the W2BF and depends on its constituency. Regular reporting back to the forum (bi-monthly meetings) keeps the project accountable and connected to the ward's other development priorities. The education/skills champion reports at every meeting.

**Ongoing: TRANCRAA and Farm 39 engagement.**
If the campus involves community land, water rights, or Farm 39 resources at any point, the Communal Property Association and the TRANCRAA process become central. This is not a Phase 0 dependency (the campus can launch on other premises), but it is a standing consideration that intensifies if the campus succeeds and seeks to expand.

**Ongoing: Relationship management with provincial and national government.**
TWKM (Stalin Govender, economic development), WCED (school registration), DHET (CET College), QCTO (SDP accreditation), CATHSSETA, AgriSETA, DEDAT (tourism), Department of Agriculture (Elsenburg): each has its own calendar, application process, and institutional logic. The NPC needs someone (staff or board member) who can manage these relationships over years, not months.

**Ongoing: Financial sustainability.**
The combined revenue model (school fees, SETA funding, CET allocation, grants, tourism income, donor/CSI funding) requires active management. No single revenue stream is sufficient. The feasibility study (0.5) models this; Phase 1 tests it; Phase 2 depends on it. A financial manager or treasurer who understands SETA claiming, grant reporting, and school fee administration is not optional.
