An integrated community learning facility combining an English-medium high school, a bioregional learning centre, and a community skills centre. Not yet constituted. Earliest high school enrolment: 2029 or 2030. Skills centre and adult education components could begin earlier.

**This profile is based on a feasibility exploration (MS) arising from the inaugural Ward 2 Business and Community Development Forum meeting (16 September 2026). No formal proposal, registration, or funding application has been made. All claims should be treated as MS or TBV.**

## The premise

A standalone English-medium high school is not viable in the valley. The learner catchment is too small to sustain the infrastructure, staffing, and fee levels a conventional independent school requires.

The learning campus model tests whether combining three functions under one institution, sharing infrastructure and staff, drawing on multiple revenue streams, and serving the whole ward rather than one community segment, creates viability that a standalone school cannot.

The model responds directly to the five development priorities identified by the Ward 2 Business and Community Development Forum: tourism, education and skills, agriculture and food sovereignty, conservation, and business development. Four of those five have an education or training component the valley currently cannot deliver locally.

## What the campus contains

The three components share a single site, a single legal entity (NPC), and, where appropriate, staff and infrastructure. They register separately under different authorities.

### The high school

A registered independent school, English-medium, CAPS-compliant, Grade 8 to 12. Small: 60 to 120 learners at maturity, starting with Grade 8 only and growing one or two year groups at a time. Registered with the WCED under SASA Section 46. Examination body to be determined.

The school's curriculum is bioregionally grounded. Geography uses the Riviersonderend watershed. History uses Genadendal's mission heritage. Life Sciences uses fynbos ecology. Tourism, Agricultural Sciences, and Business Studies draw on the valley's actual economy. This orientation is shared with the other two components and is the school's organising principle.

The small-school subject menu problem (see [[variables/07-subject-availability|Variable 7]]) is addressed through integration with the skills centre. A learner taking Tourism does practical components through a CATHSSETA-accredited guide training programme running on the same campus. A learner studying Agricultural Sciences does practical blocks through the Elsenburg-linked programme. The school's elective range is wider than its enrolment alone could support because adult skills programmes carry the infrastructure and staffing for those subjects.

### The bioregional learning centre (BLC)

A place-based education and research facility serving both the school and the wider community. The centre is designed as a node within the Valley of Grace Nature Realm (~10,000 hectares), with defined learning corridors extending into the Greyton commonage, the Greyton Nature Reserve, and the Farm 39 TRANCRAA land. The centre is the base; the valley is the campus.

It develops the bioregional curriculum modules based on what is already available internationally, runs community education programmes (heritage walks, ecological literacy, food sovereignty workshops), hosts research (2038 founding of Genadendal tricentenary, leopard corridor mapping, bioprecipitation, BioConomy documentation), and operates heritage and ecological tours as a social enterprise. Learners take stewardship responsibility for allocated restoration blocks within the Realm: wetland rehabilitation, alien clearing, erosion management, and species monitoring. The conservation area's condition becomes part of their learning record.

The BLC is what makes the school distinctive and what makes the campus a tourism destination. It is not a museum and not a conservation NGO; it operates alongside the Genadendal Mission Museum and CapeNature, creating programming that draws on both.

### The community skills centre

A QCTO-accredited Skills Development Provider offering occupational qualifications, short courses, and learnerships aligned with the valley's economy. It serves adults (community members, emerging farmers, tourism entrants, school leavers) and high school learners in integrated programmes.

The skills centre is not a TVET college. It does not require DHET establishment. It operates under QCTO accreditation and seeks programme-specific accreditation from relevant SETAs: CATHSSETA (tourism and hospitality), AgriSETA (agriculture), and others as demand emerges.

A potential addition: a CET satellite learning centre hosted under the Western Cape CET College's existing Overberg Community Learning Centre. This brings state-funded adult basic education (AET Levels 1-4, GETC equivalent to Grade 9) and serves community members who did not complete schooling, creating a pipeline into the occupational qualifications.

Programmes mapped to W2DF priorities (Ward 2 Development Forum: Business & Community) include: 
- tourist guide accreditation (cultural, nature, adventure), 
- hospitality skills, 
- agricultural occupational qualifications (in partnership with Elsenburg), 
- small-scale farmer development, 
- alien vegetation clearing and ecological restoration, 
- basic business management, and 
- digital skills.

## Timeline

The campus does not launch all three components simultaneously. The phasing is designed to build institutional capacity and demonstrate demand before committing to the most complex component (the school).

**Phase 0 (now to mid-2027):** Convene a working group through the W2DF. Engage the Transformasie Komitee. Conduct a feasibility study. Approach the Western Cape CET College, Elsenburg, and CATHSSETA about partnerships. Identify a site.

**Phase 1 (2027-2028):** Launch the CET satellite and first SETA-funded skills programmes. Begin community education through the BLC. Pursue WESSA Eco-School certification and EEASA affiliation for the campus. Generate revenue and track record.

**Phase 2 (2028-2029):** Register the high school with the WCED. Open with Grade 8 only, growing one year group per year. First matric cohort writes in 2032 or 2033.

**Phase 3 (2029-2033):** Full five-year high school. Skills centre running multiple programmes annually. BLC hosting research, visitors, and regular tourism programming.

A family making a 2028 decision cannot enrol a child in this school. A family whose child enters Grade 8 in 2028 at another school should know this option may exist by the time their child reaches Grade 10 or 11, and that the skills centre and BLC components may be operational before the school opens.

## Relationship to other options

**Emil Weder:** The campus is not a competitor. Emil Weder serves the Afrikaans-medium community. The skills centre and BLC serve everyone in the ward, including Emil Weder learners who want supplementary programmes in tourism, agriculture, or conservation.

**Silverwood:** The campus model is broader than Silverwood (which is proposed as a school only). If Silverwood proceeds, the two could coexist, merge, or one could absorb the other. The campus model addresses the viability problem that a standalone school faces; whether Silverwood's backers see that as complementary or competing is an open question.

**Online schooling and microschool:** During Phase 0 and Phase 1, families who choose online schooling or a microschool for 2028 could use the BLC and skills centre as supplementary resources, effectively building the "partial option" layer that the Decision Tool's structure anticipates.

## Best fit

The campus model suits families who want English-medium, in-valley schooling and are willing to wait for it to be established; families who value education grounded in the valley's ecology, heritage, and economy; and families for whom an institution serving the whole ward (not just one demographic segment) matters.

It suits the Genadendal community because the skills centre and CET satellite serve adult education and employment needs directly, and because the governance model is designed to represent the ward, not one village.

It does not suit families who need a school operating by 2028. It does not suit families who want a proven institution with a matric track record before enrolling. It carries all the risks of a start-up institution, compounded by the complexity of a combined model.

## Key unknowns

- Whether the W2DF working group can be convened with adequate Genadendal representation. The protocol concern raised at the inaugural meeting is foundational. **TBV** through the November 2026 W2DF meeting.
- Whether the Western Cape CET College would host a satellite learning centre in Genadendal. **TBV** with the WC CET College directly.
- Whether Elsenburg would partner on satellite delivery of agricultural occupational qualifications, with valley commercial farmers as host farms. **TBV** with Elsenburg and with local farmers.
- Whether CATHSSETA accreditation as a training provider is obtainable for a new NPC. **TBV** with CATHSSETA and WCG.
- Site. Where the campus is located determines whose institution it is perceived to be. No site has been identified. **TBV** through community process.
- Minimum viable enrolment and revenue. The combined revenue model has not been costed. **TBV** through feasibility study.
- Relationship to Silverwood. **TBV** through direct conversation with Silverwood proponents.
- The TRANCRAA process on Farm 39. If the campus involves community land or water rights, the Communal Property Association and the TRANCRAA process become central. **TBV** with the Transformasie Komitee and DALRRD.
