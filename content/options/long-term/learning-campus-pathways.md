This page follows a young person from the valley out of high school. It sets out what is within reach for a school leaver today, then what the proposed [[options/long-term/learning-campus|Valley of Grace Learning Campus]] could add if it is built as planned. The largest addition is local work and study in the bioregional economy: restoring the valley's catchments, guiding visitors, producing food and running the cooperatives that hold this work together.

> [!warning] This page describes a best case
> Everything under *The best case* assumes that the campus goes ahead in full: an operator runs the English-medium boarding school, the WCED establishes the school of skills, the vocational training centre gains QCTO and SETA accreditation, and the Bioregional Learning Centre finds a university partner. None of this exists yet. Every claim about the campus is [MS] or [TBV], and the earliest high school intake is 2030 [TBV]. A family choosing a school for 2028 should plan around the first section, *Leaving school in the valley today*.

## Leaving school in the valley today

Emil Weder Secondary in Genadendal is the valley's only operating high school [IC]. It is an Afrikaans-medium public school writing the DBE National Senior Certificate (NSC). A Schools Digest listing reports it as a Quintile 3 no-fee school with 588 learners and 21 educators in 2023 [TBV: unofficial listing; confirm with the WCED]. Its recent matric pass and bachelor-pass rates have not been confirmed for this site [TBV].

The NSC from Emil Weder carries the same standing as any other NSC [IC]. What limits a school leaver is where the next step is, and what it costs to get there.

| Route | Where | Entry | Cost to the learner | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| University degree | Stellenbosch, Cape Town or further | NSC Bachelor pass; many science programmes need Mathematics and Physical Sciences | Tuition, accommodation and living costs; NSFAS covers qualifying students at public universities | [IC] |
| TVET college | Boland TVET College, Caledon campus, about 35 km from Greyton | Grade 9 for NCV Level 2; Grade 12 for NATED N4 | NSFAS funds qualifying students; daily transport | [IC] |
| Caledon campus programmes | Caledon | As above | As above | Office Administration, Management, Tourism and Early Childhood Development [IC]. Engineering and agricultural technology are taught at Worcester and Strand [IC]. |
| Agricultural occupational certificate | Elsenburg, Stellenbosch, about 120 km away, with farm blocks on a host farm | Grade 9 or Grade 10, with English and Mathematical Literacy, and access to a host farm | Free, with hostel, meals and a stipend; the learner pays transport | [IC] |
| Tourist guide qualification | Training providers in Cape Town or online; none in the Overberg | Grade 12 and two South African languages | R6,500 to R18,900 (provider prices, 2026), plus R240 registration | [IC] |
| Direct work | The valley's tourism, farming, construction and services | None | None | Mostly informal or seasonal [TBV] |

Three facts follow from the table [AI]:

- Every route to a post-school qualification starts outside the valley. The closest is 35 km away, and most are over 100 km.
- The Caledon campus offers no programme in conservation, ecological restoration or agriculture. The valley's conservation estate and farms sit on its doorstep, yet a school leaver who wants to train for that work must go to Elsenburg or a university.
- University study in conservation needs strong school Mathematics and Physical Sciences. Stellenbosch University's BSc in Conservation Ecology asks for Mathematics at 60% and Physical Sciences at 50% [MS: su.ac.za]. Whether Emil Weder offers Physical Sciences in Grades 10 to 12 is unconfirmed [TBV].

The wider picture is hard. In the first quarter of 2026, 60.9% of South Africans aged 15 to 24 in the labour force were unemployed, and 37.6% of that age group were not in employment, education or training [IC: Stats SA, QLFS Q1 2026]. No valley-level figure exists [TBV].

## The best case: what the Learning Campus adds

If the campus is built as the [[options/long-term/learning-campus-design-brief|design brief]] proposes, a school leaver has a place to continue inside the valley. The campus holds four parts, and three of them serve school leavers directly [MS].

| Part | Who it serves after school | What it offers |
|---|---|---|
| Vocational training centre | School leavers from every valley school, and adults | QCTO occupational qualifications and SETA learnerships: tourist guiding, hospitality, agriculture with Elsenburg, alien clearing and ecological restoration, business management, digital skills [MS] |
| Bioregional Learning Centre | Graduates of all the valley's schools and of the training centre | Higher NQF occupational qualifications, field programmes, research, and degree study through a university partner [MS; university partner TBV] |
| CET satellite | Adults who left school early | State-funded adult education up to the GETC (equivalent to Grade 9), which opens the occupational qualifications [MS; CET College agreement TBV] |

Emil Weder learners are invited to use the training centre and the Bioregional Learning Centre on equal terms [MS]. The campus objective is that by 2038 every school leaver in Ward 2 is in work, study or training within a year of leaving school [MS].

The boarding school adds one further route. Its strongest learners, and strong learners from other valley schools, could apply to United World Colleges for the IB Diploma after Grade 10 or 11. The [[research/uwc-research-brief|research report on a route to the IB Diploma]] sets out how that could work and what remains open.

## The bioregional economy

The BioConomy wiki defines bioregional economics as "the study and practice of designing regenerative, cooperatively owned, place-based economies within the carrying capacity of their landscapes" [MS: [BioConomy wiki](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/bioregional-economics)]. For a school leaver, the practical meaning is paid work that restores and uses the valley's land and water, organised through local cooperatives and paid for by buyers who value the results.

This economy already pays for work in the Western Cape:

- **Catchment restoration.** The Greater Cape Town Water Fund, launched in 2018, cleared more than 46,000 hectares of invasive trees by October 2023. It reports 722 green job opportunities since 2019, nearly half held by women, including about 150 high-angle rope technicians [MS: The Nature Conservancy]. Its target areas include the Theewaterskloof catchment [MS]. Theewaterskloof Dam sits upstream of the valley on the same river, the Riviersonderend [TBV]. The valley's own slopes therefore drain below the dam and do not feed Cape Town's supply [AI].
- **Outcome-based finance.** In April 2026, FirstRand Bank (through RMB) issued a R2.5 billion bond whose returns to investors depend on verified restoration of Western Cape water catchments. It was listed on the JSE. The Western Cape Government said the project would provide employment for rural communities, targeting women and youth [IC: Western Cape Government statement, 14 April 2026; JSE; FSD Africa].
- **Fynbos harvesting.** On the Agulhas Plain in the Overberg, the Flower Valley Conservation Trust's sustainable harvesting programme covered 75,000 hectares in 2016 and benefited about 200 families. A linked alien clearing programme employed 250 people over three years [MS: Flower Valley case study, December 2017].
- **Guiding.** Only 720 tourist guides are registered in the Western Cape, and guiding for payment without the qualification is illegal [MS: W2BF meeting, 16 September 2026].

The wiki argues that a bioregion gains a lasting income when it sells a verified ecological service to a buyer who already pays for one [MS: BioConomy wiki]. The Cape Water bond shows that such buyers exist for catchment work [IC]. To earn from them, valley teams would have to deliver verified results at scale, either in the Theewaterskloof catchment or for a buyer who values restoration below the dam [AI].

### Work this could create in the valley

| Work | What it involves | Training route on the campus | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restoration team member or team leader | Alien clearing, wetland rehabilitation, erosion control | Training centre (restoration programme); Bioregional Learning Centre restoration blocks | Demand [MS: TNC]; campus route [MS] |
| Rope-access clearing technician | Clearing invasive trees on steep mountain slopes | Training centre, with an accredited rope-access provider | Demand [MS: TNC]; route [TBV] |
| Ecological monitor | Species surveys, water and soil data, [[glossary/bioscore|BioScore]] records that buyers can verify | Bioregional Learning Centre field programmes | [MS] |
| Nature, culture or adventure guide | Guiding in the [[glossary/nature-realm|Nature Realm]], at the Genadendal Mission Museum and on heritage trails | Training centre (CATHSSETA tourist guiding) | [IC] qualification; campus provider [TBV] |
| Farm supervisor or food producer | Livestock, orchards, food gardens, supply to the campus kitchen and local market | Training centre with Elsenburg; host farms in the valley | [MS] |
| Fynbos harvester or processor | Certified sustainable harvesting and value-added products | No route planned yet | Precedent [MS: Flower Valley]; valley route [TBV] |
| Hospitality worker | Guest houses, restaurants, events, the boarding school's kitchen | Training centre (hospitality) | [MS] |
| Cooperative member or manager | Running a small enterprise within a federated cooperative, or keeping the accounts of a [[glossary/commitment-pooling|commitment pool]] | Training centre (business management); the campus [[options/long-term/learning-campus-economics|economics module]] | [MS] |
| Researcher or technician | Heritage research for the 2038 tercentenary, leopard corridor mapping, bioprecipitation studies | Bioregional Learning Centre with a university partner | [MS]; partner [TBV] |

Most of these roles can start at an occupational certificate and grow with experience and further study [AI]. A school leaver with a strong NSC in Mathematics and Life Sciences could also go on to a degree in conservation ecology, hydrology or environmental science, then return to the valley in a professional role [AI].

## What has to be true

The best case depends on conditions that are not yet in place [AI]:

- An operator signs a lease for the boarding school, and its rent and levy fund the public parts of the campus [TBV].
- The vocational training centre gains QCTO accreditation and SETA programme approval [TBV].
- A buyer, such as a water fund or a bond-funded programme, contracts for restoration work that valley teams can reach [TBV].
- Valley employers pledge placements and jobs [TBV].
- A university agrees to partner with the Bioregional Learning Centre [TBV].
- The campus trust represents the whole ward, including Genadendal, and Emil Weder's governing body chooses to take part [TBV].

## Key unknowns

- Emil Weder's matric pass rate, bachelor-pass rate and Grade 10 to 12 subject list, including Physical Sciences. **TBV** with the school or the WCED.
- Where Emil Weder learners go after school today, and how many are in work, study or training a year later. **TBV** through a tracer survey, possibly run with the school.
- Whether the Greater Cape Town Water Fund or the Cape Water bond programme would contract restoration work in the Theewaterskloof catchment through a valley provider. **TBV** with The Nature Conservancy South Africa.
- Whether any buyer would pay for restoration below Theewaterskloof Dam, where the valley's own land drains, for example farmers downstream or a river health programme. **TBV** with the Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency and valley farmers.
- Whether Boland TVET College would run a conservation, agriculture or hospitality programme at a valley site. **TBV** with Boland TVET College.
- Whether CapeNature would recognise campus training for work in its reserves. **TBV** with CapeNature.
- Which university would partner with the Bioregional Learning Centre, and on what terms. **TBV** with Stellenbosch University and Rhodes University's Environmental Learning Research Centre.
- How many valley residents currently work in restoration, guiding, farming and hospitality. **TBV** through the W2BF.
