A CATHSSETA-accredited qualification that allows the holder to work legally as a tourist guide in South Africa. The qualification is regulated: guiding without it is illegal. The valley's tourism economy is growing (24% month-on-month at the time of the W2BF meeting), the Western Cape Government has set a target of doubling inbound tourists by 2030, and only 720 registered tourist guides serve the entire province. This profile covers the training pathway, not a specific training provider, because the choice of provider depends on budget, location, and specialisation.

This profile was prompted by discussion at the inaugural W2BF meeting (16 September 2026), where tourism was identified as the ward's first development priority, and the shortage of registered guides was raised as both a bottleneck and an opportunity.

## The qualification

**FET Certificate: Tourist Guiding**
SAQA ID 71549. NQF Level 4. Minimum 134 credits.

This is the standard qualification for tourist guides in South Africa. It is issued by CATHSSETA-accredited training providers and registered on the National Qualifications Framework. Without it, a person cannot legally conduct guided tours for payment.

The qualification covers three classes of guiding, each requiring specific unit standards:

- **Culture guiding**: museums, heritage sites, community tours, wine farms, city tours.
- **Nature guiding**: game reserves, national parks, conservation areas.
- **Adventure guiding**: rock climbing, abseiling, paddling, and other outdoor activities.

And three categories, based on geographic scope:

- **Site guide**: limited to a specific geographical area (a hiking trail, a city, a heritage site).
- **Provincial guide**: authorised to conduct tours throughout an entire province.
- **National guide**: permitted to lead tours across all provincial boundaries.

A site guide qualification requires specific unit standard combinations aligned with the specialisation. A provincial or national guide requires the full NQF Level 4 qualification plus the relevant unit standards.

For the valley, the most immediately relevant combination is a **culture site guide** qualification covering the Western Cape, which would authorise guiding at the Genadendal Mission Museum, along heritage trails, through the village, and across the Overberg. 

## Entry requirements

- Grade 12 (matric) or equivalent.
- Proficiency in two South African languages.
- English fluency.
- Reading, writing, and basic mathematical skills.
- South African citizenship or valid work permit.

The Grade 12 requirement is significant. Unlike the Elsenburg agricultural qualifications (which accept from Grade 9), tourist guide training requires matric. This means the qualification is post-school only: a learner must complete their schooling before entering the programme.

## Training providers (Western Cape focus)

Three CATHSSETA-accredited providers serve the Western Cape market. Costs and formats differ considerably.

**Livingstone Tourism Academy** (Cape Town)
Accreditation: 613/P/000222/2013.
Course: Culture Site Tour Guide.
Duration: 4 weeks.
Cost: R6,500.
Intakes: September (rolling).

**Tourist Guide Institute** (Century City, Cape Town)
Accreditation: 613/P/000053/2005.
Course: Culture Site Guide Level 4 (SAQA Tg/CultSiteGd/4/0031).
Duration: 2 weeks full-time contact plus 5 weeks self-study. Online option: 5-8 weeks theory plus 4.5 days practical.
Cost: R9,100.
Coverage: Western Cape sub-regions including Overberg and Breede River Valley.
Intakes: Four per year (January, April, June, September).

**Drum Beat Academy** (Pretoria/Johannesburg, with online option)
Course: FET Certificate: Tourist Guiding (SAQA 71549).
Duration: 6-12 months (online) or 5 days contact plus self-study.
Cost: R12,600 (one province) to R18,900 (nine provinces). Online: R11,900 to R18,400.
Coverage: Flexible by province.

For a valley resident, the Tourist Guide Institute is the strongest fit: it is Cape Town-based, its curriculum explicitly covers the Overberg and Breede River Valley, and its online option reduces time away from home to under a week.

## After training: registration

Completing the training course is not enough. The graduate must register with the provincial registrar before they can work legally.

**Western Cape Provincial Registrar**
Department of Economic Development and Tourism (DEDAT)
Ground Floor, NBS Waldorf Arcade, 80 St George's Mall, Cape Town
Phone: 0860 142 142 (Option 3)
Email: Registrar.Registrar@westerncape.gov.za

Registration requires:
- CATHSSETA Certificate of Competence
- Valid first aid certificate (from a Department of Labour-recognised institution)
- Signed code of conduct
- Certified SA identity document
- Two passport photos
- R240 registration fee

Registration is valid for three years. Renewal costs R240.

## Cost

This is a self-funded qualification unless a learnership or bursary is secured.

**Estimated cost for a Greyton resident (Tourist Guide Institute, online option):**

| Component | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Course fee | R9,100 |
| First aid certificate (obtained separately) | R800 - R1,500 |
| Transport to Cape Town (practical days) | R500 - R1,000 |
| Provincial registration | R240 |
| **Total** | **R10,640 - R11,840** |

The Livingstone Academy route costs roughly R8,000 all-in. The Drum Beat Academy online route costs R13,000 or more.

**Funding possibilities:**

CATHSSETA funds learnerships through employers and accredited training providers. A learnership would cover the training cost and provide a monthly stipend (reported in the range of R3,500 per month for general CATHSSETA learnerships). Learnership availability is not guaranteed: CATHSSETA allocates funding to qualifying employers and providers who then recruit learners. A tourism operator in the valley who wanted to train a guide could apply to host a learnership. This has not been tested locally. The W2BF Tourism Growth Fund application (approximately R1.4 million, submitted to the Western Cape Tourism Growth Fund) includes a community heritage guide training component, which could provide a funding vehicle if the grant is awarded.

## Why this matters for the high school decision

This profile sits in the partial options section because tourist guide training is not a school. It does not replace the high school decision. But it changes the post-school calculation in three ways.

First, it is an income-generating qualification accessible immediately after matric. A school leaver who completes the culture guide course (4-8 weeks, R6,500-R9,100) can register and begin earning within months. For families where the cost of a three-year university degree is prohibitive, this is a concrete alternative that connects to the valley's growing tourism economy.

Second, it intersects with the W2BF's first development priority. The forum identified the shortage of registered guides as a bottleneck. The valley has the assets (Genadendal is the oldest mission station in South Africa, a national heritage site; the surrounding mountains, fynbos, and leopard corridor are conservation tourism draws), but it lacks the qualified people to turn those assets into guided experiences. Every trained guide from the valley adds capacity to an industry the entire ward has agreed to develop.

Third, for a learner at any school who takes Tourism as a CAPS subject, the tourist guide qualification adds a practical, regulated credential to what is otherwise a theoretical subject. This is relevant to the proposed Valley of Grace Learning Campus (see [[options/long-term/learning-campus|long-term options]]), where tourism training could be integrated into the skills centre component from the outset.

The qualification also serves adults. A community member of any age with matric can enter the programme. Combined with the CET satellite proposal in the Learning Campus model (where an adult without matric could complete GETC through the Community Education and Training programme), this creates a two-step pathway: GETC, then tourist guide qualification, then employment.

## Best fit

This qualification suits school leavers with matric who are interested in tourism, heritage, or the outdoors and want a short, affordable route to employment. It suits adults with matric who are looking for a career change or an additional income stream. It suits anyone in the valley who wants to turn the community's heritage and natural assets into a livelihood.

It does not suit learners who have not completed matric (they need to finish school or complete a GETC first). It does not suit people with no interest in working with visitors. The training cost (R6,500 to R18,900) is a barrier for families without savings, though CATHSSETA learnerships and the TGF grant could offset this for some candidates.
