English-medium independent primary school in Greyton, ISASA member, currently operating Grade R to Grade 7. The school's WCED registration already covers Grades 8 and 9. The principal has stated that offering these grades is an option worth considering, subject to board clearance and accommodation.

## Variable-by-variable assessment

### 1. Language of instruction

English-medium. This is confirmed through the school's existing primary operation (IC: ISASA Schoolfinder listing, school website). Children already enrolled at Greyton House for primary school would continue in their current language of instruction.

### 2. Daily logistics

Greyton House is in Greyton village. No commute to Caledon. No boarding required. The child continues attending school in the same location as primary school.

For families whose children already attend Greyton House, the logistical cost of continuing into Grade 8 is zero: same school, same route, same drop-off. For other Greyton families, it is a short trip within the village.

This is the simplest logistical outcome for any English-speaking Greyton family.

### 3. School type and registration

Registered independent school. ISASA member (IC: ISASA Schoolfinder listing). The school's WCED registration already covers Grades 8 and 9 (MS: principal's statement). This means the regulatory groundwork for offering senior phase is in place. The school does not need to apply for new registration to open these grades.

Two conditions remain before enrolment can proceed: board clearance (governance approval from the school's governing body) and accommodation (physical space for additional classes and learners). Both are TBV with the school.

The registration covers Grade 8 and 9 only. It does not extend to the FET phase (Grades 10-12). A learner completing Grade 9 at Greyton House would need to transfer to another school for Grade 10 onwards. This is a structural limitation, though it aligns with the fact that Grade 9 is a natural transition point in the South African system (the end of the senior phase and the point at which TVET entry becomes possible).

### 4. Curriculum and examination body

The primary school follows the CAPS curriculum (IC: school website). It is reasonable to expect that Grade 8-9 would also follow CAPS, since the WCED registration would require curriculum compliance. The examination body for internal senior-phase assessment has not been specified. At Grade 8-9 level (before the FET phase), assessment is school-based under CAPS and does not involve an external examination body.

### 5. Cost

Current primary school fees have not been confirmed for this profile. As a registered independent school and ISASA member, fees will be higher than a public school. The fee level for Grade 8-9, if offered, has not been set or announced.

Fee exemptions or bursaries: TBV. Independent schools sometimes offer partial bursaries, but availability and criteria vary.

### 6. Pedagogy and values

Greyton House operates as a small village school. The primary school's pedagogical approach is described on the school's curriculum page, but the specifics were not retrievable during this research session. As a small independent school in a rural village, the teaching environment is likely to feature small class sizes, close teacher-learner relationships, and some degree of flexibility in delivery.

Whether the school would adopt a distinctive senior-phase pedagogy (project-based, place-based, or Waldorf-influenced) or deliver standard CAPS instruction is not yet known. This is a question for the principal and the board.

Connection to the valley's ecology and heritage: as a Greyton-based school, there is proximity. Whether this translates into curriculum content depends on how the senior phase is designed. The Ward 2 Schools Program's supplementary modules could potentially be offered here, as at Emil Weder. TBV with the programme coordinator and the school.

### 7. Subject availability

Not yet specified. At Grade 8-9 (senior phase), CAPS requires: two languages, Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy, Natural Sciences, Social Sciences, Technology, Economic and Management Sciences, Life Orientation, and Creative Arts. A small school may deliver all of these with a small teaching team, though specialist subjects (Natural Sciences, Technology) require qualified staff.

The subject menu at Grade 8-9 does not yet involve FET elective choices (those come at Grade 10). The question is whether Greyton House can staff the full senior-phase CAPS programme with qualified teachers.

### 8. Post-school pathway

Greyton House's registration covers Grade 8 and 9 only. A learner completing Grade 9 would need to transfer to another school for the FET phase (Grades 10-12) in order to write the NSC. Transfer destinations would include Overberg High (Caledon), an online provider, a boarding school, or (if constituted) Silverwood.

This two-stage pathway (Grades 8-9 at Greyton House, Grades 10-12 elsewhere) has a parallel in Waldorf schools, where learners sometimes complete Class 12 at one school and write matric exams through another. It is not unusual, but it requires planning.

Boland TVET College's Caledon campus accepts learners from Grade 9, so a child completing Grade 9 at Greyton House could also enter TVET directly.

### 9. Social and community considerations

The child's peer group remains in Greyton. For children already at Greyton House primary, this is social continuity: same friends, same village, same community. For other Greyton children, it offers an in-valley English-medium peer group.

The school's existing community is predominantly English-speaking Greyton families. The socioeconomic and demographic profile is narrower than Emil Weder's. Whether a Grade 8-9 cohort would draw learners from Genadendal or the wider valley, broadening the mix, depends on fees, outreach, and transport.

If enough families commit to Greyton House for Grade 8-9, the cohort also becomes a natural feeder group for collective solutions at Grade 10: a shared transition to Overberg High, a joint online-schooling arrangement, or the critical mass needed for a microschool or Silverwood's FET phase.

### 10. Silverwood-specific

Greyton House offering Grade 8-9 addresses part of the same need that Silverwood aims to fill: in-valley, English-medium secondary schooling. The two are potential complements (Greyton House for Grade 8-9, Silverwood for FET) or competitors (if both aim to serve the same small pool of families). The relationship between the two proposals is a question for the planning session.

Key differences: Greyton House is an existing, registered, operating school with an established track record. Silverwood is proposed and not yet constituted. Greyton House covers Grade 8-9 only. Silverwood aims to cover Grade 8 through matric.

## Best fit

Greyton House for Grade 8-9 suits families where: the child already attends Greyton House and the family wants continuity; English-medium, in-valley schooling is the priority; the family is comfortable with a two-stage pathway (Grades 8-9 locally, Grades 10-12 elsewhere); the fees are within budget.

It is a weaker fit where: the family needs a single school through to matric; the family requires FET subject certainty from the outset; the child's peer group or social formation needs to be sustained through Grade 12 without a school transfer.

## Key unknowns

- Board clearance: whether the governing body approves offering Grade 8-9. **TBV** with the school board.
- Accommodation: whether the school has or can secure the physical space for senior-phase classes. **TBV** with the school.
- Timeline: earliest start date for Grade 8 enrolment (2028 or later). **TBV** with the principal.
- Fees: what Grade 8-9 fees would be. **TBV** with the school.
- Staffing: whether the school can recruit or reassign teachers qualified for senior-phase CAPS subjects (Natural Sciences, Mathematics, Social Sciences, Technology). **TBV** with the school.
- Minimum enrolment: how many Grade 8 learners are needed to make the offering viable. **TBV** with the school.
- Grade 10 pathway: what the school recommends or facilitates for learners completing Grade 9. **TBV** with the principal.
- Current primary school fees (as a baseline indicator). **TBV** via the school's admissions office or website.
