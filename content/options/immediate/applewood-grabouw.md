Independent school in Grabouw (Elgin Valley). Currently a preparatory school (Grade R to 7), with an announced expansion into high school from 2027. Founded in 1998. English-medium. Small school (~97 to 136 learners across all grades). Inquiry-based, outdoor-oriented pedagogy in the Kogelberg Biosphere Reserve. Approximately 80 km from Greyton via the N2 over Houw Hoek Pass.

All high school information below is preliminary. The school's primary programme is well established; the secondary programme has been announced but details (curriculum, examination body, subject range, fees, staffing, registration status for Grades 8+) have not been publicly confirmed. Every claim about the high school phase is **TBV** directly with the school.

## Variable-by-variable assessment

### 1. Language of instruction

English is the medium of instruction. Afrikaans is offered at home language level. This meets both language communities, though the school environment is predominantly English.

### 2. Daily logistics

Greyton to Grabouw is approximately 80 km via the N2 over the Houw Hoek Pass. Driving time is roughly 1 hour to 1 hour 15 minutes. The route crosses the same Houw Hoek area as the Caledon road but continues further west.

This is not a viable daily commute by a single family. Whether the school will operate a bus service, or whether a scholar transport route from the Overberg side exists or could be established, is unknown.

**Transport cost (if driving solo):** Comparable to or greater than the Overberg High solo-driver estimate (~R44,000+/year in fuel alone, plus ~300+ hours of driving time). A lift club with families from Caledon or the Houw Hoek corridor could reduce this, but no such arrangement has been confirmed.

The school address is 10 Appletiser Road, Grabouw 7180.

School hours for the primary school are not confirmed for the high school phase. **TBV.**

### 3. School type and registration

Independent school, WCED-registered for primary (Grade R to 7). The school has been operating since 1998, which gives it a 27-year institutional track record at the primary level.

Whether the high school phase will be registered as a separate school or as an extension of the existing registration is unknown. WCED registration for Grades 8 and above requires a separate application and approval process. **TBV** whether registration has been applied for or granted.

The school is privately governed (not SGB-governed). The founding board structure dates to 1997. Current governance arrangements have not been publicly detailed.

### 4. Curriculum and examination body

The primary school uses an inquiry-based, CAPS-aligned curriculum. One external source lists the curriculum as Cambridge International; the school's own website describes it as progressive and environment-based without naming a specific framework. This discrepancy should be clarified directly with the school.

The high school curriculum and examination body have not been announced. The options are CAPS/NSC (via DBE), CAPS/IEB, or Cambridge International (IGCSE/AS/A-Level). The choice matters for university eligibility, subject comparability, and cost. **TBV.**

### 6. Pedagogy and values

This is the school's strongest differentiator. Applewood describes its approach as "innovative, dynamic, individualised learning in an outdoor environment." The curriculum is environment-based, with learning connected to the Kogelberg Biosphere Reserve and the Elgin Valley landscape. The school emphasises creative, critical, and strategic thinking, with children given "freedom to play and explore in the unique countryside environment."

Core values: integrity, grit, effort, compassion, respect, self-control, commitment, initiative, gratitude, and kindness. The motto is "Good Roots, Good Fruits."

Facilities include a 25-metre swimming pool (Aquatic Centre, opened 2018), mountain bike track and skills park, two sports ovals, cricket nets, a music block, computer lab, library, and learning support centre. Outdoor education is central: camps, eco-walks, and a Grade 7 five-day Mountain2Coast Challenge.

The learner-to-educator ratio is 5.7:1, which is exceptionally low. With ~97 to 136 learners and 17 to 28 staff (including assistants), this is a small, high-contact environment.

Whether the high school phase will maintain the same pedagogical approach, or shift toward a more conventional academic model to meet examination requirements, is a key question. **TBV.**

For families attracted to place-based, nature-connected education, this school's philosophy is closer to what Silverwood proposes than any other established option on this list, though it is located outside the valley.

### 7. Subject availability

High school subject range has not been announced. As a new high school with a small intake, the initial subject offering is likely to be limited. This is the typical constraint for any new or small independent school: specialist teachers for Physical Sciences, IT, or niche electives may not be viable until learner numbers grow.

The primary school offers music, drama (Helen O'Grady Academy partnership), visual arts, and a range of sports (swimming, mountain biking, trail running, soccer, hockey, netball, tennis, softball, tag rugby). Whether these carry into the high school programme is unknown.

**TBV:** full FET subject list, including whether extended or digital subject options will be available.

### 8. Post-school pathway

Entirely dependent on the examination body the school chooses for high school. IEB and Cambridge are the most common choices for independent schools in the Western Cape. NSC (via DBE) is possible but less typical for a school with this pedagogical profile.

Until the examination body is confirmed, it is not possible to assess post-school pathway strength. **TBV.**

### 9. Social and community considerations

Applewood is a small school with a strong community ethos. The current learner body of ~97 to 136 children means the high school intake will likely start very small, possibly 10 to 20 learners per grade in the first years. This creates a tight peer group but a narrow social pool.

The school community is based in the Grabouw/Elgin area. A Greyton child's social life would centre there during school hours, with valley connections maintained through evenings and weekends (assuming daily commuting rather than accommodation).

The school's outdoor and adventure programmes (camps, Mountain2Coast Challenge, mountain biking) provide strong social bonding experiences. For a child who responds to small-group, nature-connected environments, this could be a good fit. For a child who wants a large, bustling school with extensive sport fixtures and inter-school competition, the small size may feel limiting.

### 10. Silverwood-specific

Not applicable, though Applewood's pedagogical approach shares common ground with Silverwood's stated vision: inquiry-based, environment-connected, small-class learning. If Silverwood does not launch, Applewood's high school may represent the nearest existing alternative for families attracted to that model, at the cost of the daily commute.

## Best fit

This school suits families where: the family values inquiry-based, outdoor-oriented, small-class education; the budget can sustain estimated R100,000 to R170,000/year (TBV); the family can solve the Greyton-to-Grabouw transport problem; and the family is willing to enrol in a new high school programme in its first years of operation.

It is a poor fit where: the family wants an established high school with a track record of matric results; the family needs a wide FET subject range from day one; the commute from Greyton is unworkable; or the family wants the child to remain socially connected to the valley during the week.
